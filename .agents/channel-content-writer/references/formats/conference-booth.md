# Conference Booth Format Playbook

Use this format for conference booths, expo tables, sponsor stands, demo kiosks, recruiting booths, partner marketplace booths, and lightweight tabling activations. Load `../channels/events-sales.md`, `event-attendance.md`, and `event-promotion-follow-up.md` first.

## Source Guidance

- Cvent event marketing guide: https://www.cvent.com/en/blog/events/event-marketing-guide
- Cvent event budgeting guide: https://www.cvent.com/en/blog/events/event-budgeting-guide
- FTC endorsement guidance for sponsor claims and partner references: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides

## Use Cases

- Turn paid sponsor presence into qualified conversations, demos, partner intros, recruiting, customer meetings, and content capture.
- Prepare booth messaging, staffing, demo loops, qualification, lead routing, and post-event follow-up.
- Decide whether a booth, talk, side event, or attendee-only approach is the best fit.

## Required Inputs

- Booth package: location, size, furniture, power, WiFi, screen, included scans, sponsor deliverables, setup/teardown windows, shipping, storage, and organizer rules.
- Audience and goal: target accounts, roles, buyer stage, developer/community fit, recruiting goal, partner target, or product education goal.
- Message: one-line booth hook, demo promise, qualifying question, disqualifying signals, and next-step menu.
- Staffing: shifts, roles, breaks, escalation owner, technical demo owner, executive meeting owner, and end-of-day debrief.
- Assets: signage, screen loop, demo device, backup video, landing page/QR, one-pager, swag, badge scanner/app, notebooks, chargers, and cleaning supplies.
- Lead routing: tags, priority levels, required notes, owner, follow-up SLA, CRM/import process, and meeting booking path.

## Output Shape

- Booth strategy: goal, audience, message, offer, qualification model, staffing plan, and success metrics.
- Booth script: opener, qualifying questions, demo bridge, objection responses, handoff lines, and polite exit lines.
- Demo loop: 30-second, two-minute, and five-minute versions with fallback if WiFi fails.
- Asset checklist: signage, screen, QR, one-pager, giveaway, scanner, supplies, and shipping/packing list.
- Follow-up pack: priority lead message, regular lead message, partner intro message, meeting recap, and no-fit nurture.
- Retro: traffic patterns, best questions, objections, demo issues, lead quality, team fatigue, and return recommendation.

## Copy And Creative Rules

- Booth copy must be readable while walking. Use fewer words, concrete outcomes, and a visual cue that makes the category obvious.
- The opener should not trap people. Ask a useful question that lets bad-fit visitors exit gracefully.
- Use a demo ladder. Most visitors get 30 seconds; qualified visitors get two to five minutes; high-intent visitors get a booked follow-up.
- Swag should serve the strategy. It can start conversation, reward qualified time, or create memory, but should not attract only freebie traffic.
- The follow-up note must include the exact topic, demo, or problem discussed. Booth conversations blur together quickly.

## Psychological Levers

- Pattern interruption gets people to stop; relevance gets them to stay.
- Choice architecture matters. Give visitors two or three next steps, not an open-ended "let us know."
- Reciprocity should be proportional. Useful resources beat random giveaways for serious buyers or developers.
- Social proof at the booth can come from customer logos, live demos, partner presence, or speaker slots, but all claims must be approved.
- Staff energy is a conversion factor. Breaks, food, water, and clear shifts protect conversation quality.

## Platform Adaptation

- Technical conference: show real workflows, architecture, docs, API examples, and engineer credibility.
- Executive event: shorter message, sharper business outcome, private meeting path, and stronger proof.
- Recruiting booth: role clarity, team story, application path, and respectful candidate note capture.
- Partner expo: integration story, joint value, partner enablement assets, and shared follow-up owner.
- Community table: lower pressure, more education, fewer scans, and stronger trust boundaries.

## Variants And Testing

- Test booth hook, signage hierarchy, screen loop, demo length, QR destination, giveaway, opener question, and qualification tags.
- Track scans, qualified conversations, meetings booked, demos completed, target accounts reached, partner intros, follow-up completion, show-sourced opportunities, and qualitative objections.
- Debrief daily at the event. Change opener, signage, demo path, or staffing if the first day shows weak fit.
- Compare booth ROI against speaking, side events, sponsored dinners, and attendee-only meeting strategy.

## Review Checklist

- Booth goal, audience, and qualification model are explicit.
- Sponsor package constraints and deliverables are known.
- Signage, demo, QR, one-pager, scanner, supplies, and backup plan are ready.
- Staffing schedule includes breaks and escalation.
- Lead tags, notes, routing, and follow-up SLA are defined.
- Sponsor/partner/customer claims are approved.
- Retro and return/no-return decision fields are planned.

## Failure Diagnostics

- High traffic but low quality: message too broad, swag attracts wrong people, no qualifier, or event audience mismatch.
- Low traffic: poor location, unclear signage, passive staff, weak hook, or no pre-event promotion.
- Demo failures: WiFi dependency, no backup video, under-tested devices, or staff not trained.
- Follow-up misses: notes too thin, tags inconsistent, routing undefined, or messages sent too late.
- Team burnout: shifts too long, no breaks, unclear roles, or too much standing without support.

## Anti-Patterns

- Measuring booth success only by scan count.
- Starting every conversation with a pitch.
- Running a five-minute demo for every passerby.
- Letting the booth be staffed by whoever is free.
- Shipping assets without a setup checklist.
- Forgetting to book meetings before the event.

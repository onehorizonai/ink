# Podcast Episode Format Playbook

Use this format for podcast episodes, audio interviews, solo episodes, panel audio, and audio-first repurposing. Load `../channels/video-audio.md` first.

## Source Guidance

- Spotify for Creators resources: https://creators.spotify.com/resources
- YouTube creator resources when publishing video podcasts or clips: https://www.youtube.com/creators/
- FTC endorsement guidance for sponsors, affiliates, or paid guest relationships: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides

## Use Cases

- Explore expert perspective, customer story, founder narrative, tactical lesson, industry debate, product thinking, or community questions.
- Build trust through voice, depth, and recurring point of view.
- Create an anchor asset for clips, show notes, newsletters, and sales enablement.

## Required Inputs

- Show premise, episode goal, audience, host voice, guest details, and episode length.
- Core tension or question, listener payoff, proof/examples, and sponsor/disclosure needs.
- Recording format, distribution surface, clip strategy, and manual production owner.

## Output Shape

- Episode title options and one-sentence promise.
- Cold open or teaser.
- Host intro, guest intro, segment outline, questions, transitions, and closing CTA.
- Show notes, chapter timestamps placeholder, clip ideas, quote pulls, and manual production notes.

## Copy And Creative Rules

- Give the episode a thesis or central question. "Talk with guest about topic" is too weak.
- Start with a hook that demonstrates the value of listening, not a long biography.
- Sequence questions from context to stakes to stories to tactical detail to reflection.
- Ask for examples, numbers, moments, tradeoffs, and mistakes. Avoid generic advice prompts.
- Keep host transitions short and listener-oriented.
- Show notes should summarize the episode, name key topics, include links/credits, and set expectations.

## Psychological Levers

- Narrative transportation: stories and turning points hold attention.
- Authority: credibility comes from lived examples and specific tradeoffs.
- Reciprocity: give useful takeaways before asking for rating, subscribe, or CTA.
- Identity: recurring segments and language help listeners feel part of the show.
- Curiosity: teaser clips should open loops the full episode resolves.

## Platform Adaptation

- Audio-only: prioritize listening rhythm, verbal signposting, and clear descriptions.
- Video podcast: add visual beats, camera notes, clip moments, and thumbnail/title options.
- B2B demand-gen: include buyer questions, proof points, and follow-up assets without turning the episode into a pitch.
- Community podcast: include listener questions and post-episode discussion prompts.

## Variants And Testing

- Test episode titles, guest/topic mix, cold opens, episode length, clip style, and CTA placement.
- Track starts, completion rate, average consumption, followers/subscribers, clip performance, comments, shares, site visits, and conversions.
- Review drop-off points and listener feedback to sharpen pacing and topics.

## Review Checklist

- Episode has a clear promise and listener payoff.
- Questions invite specific stories and useful detail.
- Sponsor, affiliate, guest, and claim disclosures are flagged.
- Show notes, clips, and manual production tasks are included.
- The episode does not rely on the guest alone to create structure.

## Failure Diagnostics

- Low starts: title/promise too vague or guest/topic mismatch.
- Low completion: intro too long, questions generic, pacing flat, or segments unclear.
- Low clips/shares: no memorable moments or quotable insights.
- Low conversion: CTA irrelevant or buried without context.

## Anti-Patterns

- "Tell us about yourself" openings that last too long.
- Generic advice questions with no follow-up.
- Sponsor reads that hide material connections.
- Show notes that are just a transcript dump.
- No plan for clips or post-episode distribution.

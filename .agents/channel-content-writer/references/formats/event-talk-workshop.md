# Event Talk And Workshop Format Playbook

Use this format for conference talks, workshops, meetups, panels, user-group sessions, trainings, and field events. Load `../channels/events-sales.md` first.

## Source Guidance

- ON24 digital engagement resources for virtual-event measurement: https://www.on24.com/webinar-benchmarks/
- Gartner sales enablement and buyer behavior context for B2B event value: https://www.gartner.com/en/sales/topics/sales-enablement
- FTC endorsement guidance for sponsors/partners: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides

## Use Cases

- Teach a market, build authority, enable customers, support sales, train users, launch a product, or create community around a topic.
- Turn expertise into a live experience with clear attendee value.

## Required Inputs

- Event type, audience, session length, speaker, venue/platform, level, and expected interaction.
- Session goal, attendee takeaways, required assets, sponsor constraints, and CTA.
- Logistics: slides, demo, worksheet, Q&A, recording, accessibility, and follow-up owner.

## Output Shape

- Title, abstract, audience, prerequisites, and takeaways.
- Run of show with timing, sections, examples, interaction, demo/workshop steps, Q&A, and close.
- Slide outline, speaker notes, handout/worksheet ideas, promotion copy, and follow-up notes.

## Copy And Creative Rules

- Promise a specific transformation: what attendees will understand, decide, or be able to do.
- Design for the room. Talks need narrative and pacing; workshops need instructions and practice.
- Use examples and artifacts instead of abstract frameworks alone.
- Build in interaction early enough that attendees participate before the end.
- Keep sponsor/product content proportionate and disclosed.
- End with a useful next step: resource, worksheet, community, trial, meeting, or implementation checklist.

## Psychological Levers

- Public commitment can increase workshop participation when voluntary.
- Social proof appears through peer examples and questions.
- Authority comes from useful teaching and examples, not slide density.
- Ability: give attendees tools they can use immediately.
- Autonomy: offer next steps without trapping the room in a pitch.

## Platform Adaptation

- Conference talk: stronger title, narrative arc, memorable examples, and stage timing.
- Workshop: prerequisites, exercises, materials, checkpoints, and facilitator prompts.
- Panel: clear thesis, guest roles, question flow, and moderator control.
- User training: steps, demos, practice, support links, and follow-up.

## Variants And Testing

- Test title, abstract, promise, exercise format, CTA, and follow-up asset.
- Track registrations, attendance, completion, questions, resource downloads, meetings, survey scores, community joins, and pipeline/customer outcomes.
- Capture attendee questions for future content, docs, and sales enablement.

## Review Checklist

- Audience level, outcome, and session type are explicit.
- Timing, interaction, examples, and close are planned.
- Claims, sponsor/partner disclosures, customer stories, and demo risks are flagged.
- Accessibility, recording, and follow-up needs are included.
- Manual logistics are separate from content.

## Failure Diagnostics

- Low attendance: title/abstract too vague or audience mismatch.
- Low engagement: no interaction design or too much lecture.
- Poor feedback: session promise did not match delivery.
- Low conversion: CTA unrelated to attendee need or follow-up missing.

## Anti-Patterns

- Product pitches disguised as educational talks.
- Workshops with no actual work.
- Slides overloaded with text.
- Panels with no point of view.
- Ending without a useful handoff.

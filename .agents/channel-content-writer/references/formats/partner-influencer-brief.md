# Partner And Influencer Brief Format Playbook

Use this format for influencer, affiliate, partner, sponsorship, co-marketing, ambassador, creator, and referral briefs. Load `../channels/paid-partner.md` first.

## Source Guidance

- FTC endorsement guides and influencer disclosure guidance: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides
- TikTok branded content policy: https://support.tiktok.com/en/business-and-creator/creator-and-business-accounts/branded-content-policy
- Meta ad and creator guidance: https://www.facebook.com/business/ads/ad-creative
- Pinterest advertising guidelines for paid/promoted claims: https://policy.pinterest.com/en/advertising-guidelines

## Use Cases

- Brief a creator, affiliate, partner, sponsor, customer advocate, event partner, or marketplace co-marketing collaborator.
- Align creative freedom with brand, legal, disclosure, claim, and measurement requirements.

## Required Inputs

- Partner/creator, audience, platform, campaign goal, offer, compensation, usage rights, timeline, deliverables, and approval flow.
- Mandatory claims, prohibited claims, disclosure requirements, product access, tracking links/codes, and support contact.

## Output Shape

- Campaign context and audience insight.
- Deliverables, creative territory, key messages, proof points, do/don't list, disclosure language, CTA, links/codes, asset specs, timeline, approval, and reporting expectations.
- Optional creator concept prompts that leave room for native voice.

## Copy And Creative Rules

- Brief the outcome and guardrails, not a word-for-word script, unless the deliverable legally requires exact copy.
- Make disclosures clear, conspicuous, and hard to miss.
- Give creators product truth: benefits, limitations, setup, audience fit, and proof.
- Separate mandatory points from optional inspiration.
- Include realistic examples of native angles, not corporate slogans.
- Define what claims require evidence or approval.

## Psychological Levers

- Trust transfer works only when the creator's audience fit is real.
- Authenticity depends on allowing the partner to speak naturally within guardrails.
- Social proof must be disclosed and truthful.
- Reciprocity: give partners enough context and assets to make good work.
- Autonomy: over-scripting can reduce credibility and performance.

## Platform Adaptation

- Influencer/creator: native content style, disclosure, usage rights, and comment handling.
- Affiliate: clear offer, tracking, disclosure, claim limits, and compliance.
- Co-marketing: mutual audience value, co-branding, lead/list ownership, and follow-up.
- Sponsorship: placement specs, talking points, read length, deliverable review, and measurement.

## Variants And Testing

- Test creator type, angle, offer, platform, format, CTA, and landing path.
- Track reach, engagement quality, clicks, conversions, code usage, assisted conversions, content reuse value, sentiment, and compliance issues.
- Compare results by audience fit, not only follower count.

## Review Checklist

- Compensation, relationship, and disclosure needs are explicit.
- Mandatory claims and prohibited claims are clear.
- Usage rights, approvals, timeline, deliverables, and reporting are included.
- Tracking links/codes and landing-page match are planned.
- The brief allows native creative execution.

## Failure Diagnostics

- Low performance: audience mismatch, over-scripted content, weak offer, or poor landing path.
- Compliance risk: disclosure missing, unapproved claims, or unclear compensation relationship.
- Partner confusion: deliverables, timeline, or approval process incomplete.
- Low trust: creator voice suppressed or proof not believable.

## Anti-Patterns

- Asking creators to hide sponsorship.
- Paying for reach without audience fit.
- Providing scripts that sound unlike the creator.
- No claim/disclosure guardrails.
- No usage-rights or reporting terms.

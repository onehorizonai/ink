# Carousel Format Playbook

Use this format for Instagram, Facebook, Pinterest, X carousel ads, and other swipeable multi-frame social assets. Load the relevant channel guide first.

## Source Guidance

- Instagram and Meta creator guidance: https://about.fb.com/news/2024/10/best-practices-education-hub-creators-instagram/ and https://www.facebook.com/business/ads/ad-creative
- Pinterest creative best practices and product specs: https://business.pinterest.com/creative-best-practices/ and https://help.pinterest.com/en/business/article/pinterest-product-specs
- X carousel ad specifications: https://business.x.com/en/help/campaign-setup/creative-ad-specifications
- FTC endorsement guidance: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides

## Use Cases

- Teach a step-by-step concept, checklist, framework, comparison, myth-versus-fact, case snippet, product walkthrough, or event recap.
- Convert dense expertise into a saveable visual asset.
- Show multiple products, features, examples, benefits, or proof points.

## Required Inputs

- Platform, slide count or bounds, visual style, audience, and one promised payoff.
- Source material, proof, screenshots, data, customer quotes, or product views.
- Desired action: save, share, click, comment, DM, profile visit, or ad conversion.
- Brand/design constraints, safe areas, accessibility needs, and rights status.

## Output Shape

- Carousel title and caption.
- Slide-by-slide copy with visual direction for each slide.
- Alt text or accessibility notes.
- CTA slide and caption CTA, if needed.
- Manual design/export/publish notes and claim/disclosure risks.

## Copy And Creative Rules

- Slide 1 must sell the swipe with a clear outcome, tension, or curiosity gap.
- Each slide should advance the idea. Remove duplicate slides and split overloaded ones.
- Use readable mobile text. Short lines, high contrast, and one visual hierarchy per slide.
- Prefer concrete examples, diagrams, screenshots, tables, or before/after frames over decorative backgrounds.
- End with a useful action: save this, use this checklist, compare your setup, reply with a question, or visit the linked resource.
- Caption should add context and keywords, not repeat every slide.

## Psychological Levers

- Progress: numbered steps and visible completion encourage swiping.
- Cognitive fluency: simple layouts make ideas feel easier to use.
- Specificity: concrete examples increase trust.
- Reciprocity: give a genuinely useful framework before asking for action.
- Commitment: make the final action small and relevant.

Avoid manipulative cliffhangers where the payoff never arrives.

## Platform Adaptation

- Instagram: keep slide text very readable, use original visuals, and write a caption that supports saves/shares.
- Pinterest: use search-friendly titles, high-quality vertical imagery, and durable evergreen utility.
- X carousel ads: respect slide count, media specs, copy truncation, and destination behavior.
- Facebook: make the first card understandable without assuming users will swipe.

## Variants And Testing

- Test cover promise, slide count, visual proof type, CTA, and caption length.
- Track swipe-through, saves, shares, comments, clicks, conversion, and slide-level drop-off where available.
- For paid carousels, test single-destination versus multi-destination only when the platform supports it and measurement is clear.

## Review Checklist

- Slide 1 gives a real reason to continue.
- The sequence has a beginning, middle, and payoff.
- Text fits mobile and avoids UI-safe-area conflicts.
- Claims, screenshots, data, testimonials, and partner disclosures are verified or flagged.
- Caption, CTA, and manual design/export notes are included.

## Failure Diagnostics

- Low swipe-through: cover is vague, text too dense, or first slide lacks payoff.
- Low saves: content is not reusable enough.
- Low clicks: final slide/caption CTA is unclear or the offer is mismatched.
- Negative feedback: check overclaiming, missing context, accessibility, and rights.

## Anti-Patterns

- Slide decks disguised as carousels.
- Decorative cards with no new information.
- Tiny text, low contrast, or overdesigned layouts.
- Ending with a generic "follow for more".
- Splitting one paragraph into many weak slides.

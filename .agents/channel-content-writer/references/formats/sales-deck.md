# Sales Deck Format Playbook

Use this format for sales decks, discovery-to-demo narratives, pitch decks for buyers, partner decks, and enablement presentation outlines. Load `../channels/events-sales.md` first.

## Source Guidance

- Gartner sales enablement and B2B buyer behavior context: https://www.gartner.com/en/sales/topics/sales-enablement
- Gartner sales enablement overview: https://www.gartner.com/smarterwithgartner/the-who-what-how-and-why-of-sales-enablement
- FTC endorsement guidance for customer proof/testimonials: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides

## Use Cases

- Help a seller structure a buyer conversation, explain value, answer objections, show proof, support procurement, or align a buying committee.
- Create a deck outline or slide copy that can be designed manually.

## Required Inputs

- Buyer segment, deal stage, persona, problem, urgency, current solution, competitors, proof, pricing/packaging constraints, and CTA.
- Conversation type: discovery, first call, demo, business case, renewal, expansion, partner pitch, or executive readout.
- Claims, customer references, security/compliance proof, and approval needs.

## Output Shape

- Deck narrative, slide-by-slide outline, slide headlines, speaker notes, proof/visual direction, objection-handling notes, and CTA.
- Optional appendix list for security, implementation, ROI, pricing, and competitive proof.
- Manual design and approval checklist.

## Copy And Creative Rules

- Start with buyer pain and business consequence, not company history.
- Each slide headline should make a point, not label a topic.
- Use the deck to support a conversation. Do not cram every fact into the main flow.
- Put proof near the claim it supports.
- For demos, connect features to workflow moments and buyer outcomes.
- Use appendices for details that only some stakeholders need.
- Make next steps concrete: mutual action plan, pilot, security review, stakeholder intro, or procurement.

## Psychological Levers

- Buyer confidence increases when tradeoffs and risks are made explicit.
- Social proof works best when the customer proof resembles the buyer.
- Authority comes from diagnosis and evidence, not logos alone.
- Commitment: use mutual next steps rather than vague interest.
- Risk reduction: implementation, support, security, and success criteria lower friction.

## Platform Adaptation

- Executive deck: concise, business impact, risk, timing, and decision.
- Practitioner deck: workflow detail, integrations, usability, and day-to-day relief.
- Technical/security deck: architecture, permissions, data, compliance, and support.
- Partner deck: shared audience, joint value, economics, and co-selling path.

## Variants And Testing

- Test narrative order, persona-specific proof, demo depth, pricing placement, and next-step framing.
- Track stage conversion, meeting quality, stakeholder forwards, objections, deal velocity, win/loss notes, and slide usage.
- Use seller feedback and call recordings when available to refine.

## Review Checklist

- Deck is tied to a buyer stage and persona.
- Slide headlines form a coherent story.
- Claims, customer logos, numbers, and security/compliance statements are verified or flagged.
- Appendix and objection-handling needs are included.
- Manual design, legal, sales, and product approvals are clear.

## Failure Diagnostics

- Buyer confusion: deck starts with company or features before problem.
- No advancement: next step vague or not mutual.
- Objections persist: proof missing near claims or risk not addressed.
- Deck unused by sellers: too long, too generic, or not mapped to deal stage.

## Anti-Patterns

- Company-history slides at the front.
- Feature dumps with no buyer consequence.
- Logo slides without relevance.
- ROI claims without assumptions.
- No leave-behind or appendix strategy.

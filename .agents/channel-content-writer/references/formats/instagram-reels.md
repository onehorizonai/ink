## Use Cases

- Original educational, demonstrative, founder-led, behind-the-scenes, product, customer-proof, trend-adapted, or community-response video.
- Turning a recurring audience question into a saveable and shareable mobile video.
- Making abstract expertise visible through people, screens, objects, workflows, or results.

## Required Inputs

- Instagram account voice, audience, Reel objective, and desired action.
- Visual source: original footage, screen recording, product shot, demo, interview clip, B-roll, or generated concept.
- Audio plan and rights status.
- Cover frame concept, caption topic, disclosures, and manual publish owner.

## Output Shape

- Reel concept and why it fits Instagram.
- First-frame direction and hook line.
- Beat-by-beat script with timing.
- Shot list, on-screen text, caption/subtitle guidance, cover concept, caption, hashtags if relevant, and CTA.
- Manual publish QA: audio rights, cover, tags, collaboration labels, paid-partnership label, safe area, and analytics.

## Copy And Creative Rules

- Reels need immediate visual specificity. Start with an action, result, mistake, contrast, or recognizable work moment.
- Make the cover useful enough to work on the profile grid and in discovery.
- Keep overlay text short and outside UI-covered areas. Use captions for accessibility.
- Use original footage or meaningfully transformed assets. Avoid watermarked reposts and low-effort aggregation.
- Caption should support the Reel with context, keywords, and a clean CTA. It should not be required to understand the video.
- For B2B, show the real artifact: dashboard, workflow, teardown, doc, email, meeting moment, or customer result.

## Psychological Levers

- Recognition: show a pain the viewer has actually experienced.
- Progress: use numbered mistakes, steps, or before/after arcs when natural.
- Reciprocity: give a complete micro-lesson or useful example.
- Identity: let the viewer feel "this account gets my world."
- Commitment: ask for save, share, DM, or profile click only when earned.

Do not use fake "POV", false urgency, or borrowed creator formats that misrepresent the brand.

## Platform Adaptation

- Reels differ from TikTok: prioritize Instagram-original visual clarity, account aesthetic, and cover-grid coherence.
- Reels differ from Shorts: captions and profile behavior matter more; title metadata matters less.
- Reels can also be used in ads, but paid use needs stronger branding, specs, disclosures, and creative testing notes.

## Variants And Testing

- Test first frame, hook wording, cover style, length, voiceover versus talking head, overlay text density, and CTA.
- Track retention, completion, replays, shares, saves, follows, profile visits, DMs, and comment quality.
- Record whether the Reel used original footage, trend/audio, founder face, product screen, customer proof, or educational overlay.

## Review Checklist

- The first frame gives a reason to stop.
- The Reel can be understood with sound off if needed.
- Cover, caption, CTA, safe area, and manual publish notes are included.
- Rights, audio, customer data, disclosures, and claims are verified or flagged.
- The idea is Instagram-native, not a pasted TikTok or LinkedIn monologue.

## Failure Diagnostics

- Low retention: first frame too generic, too much setup, or weak visual movement.
- Low saves/shares: lesson is too shallow or not reusable.
- Low follows/profile visits: account promise is not clear after watching.
- Low comments/DMs: CTA is generic or topic does not invite response.

## Anti-Patterns

- Watermarked reposts, borrowed trend templates, and unoriginal compilations.
- Tiny overlay text and cluttered safe areas.
- Captions that do the work the video should do.
- Generic "watch until the end" hooks.
- Polished but empty brand montages.

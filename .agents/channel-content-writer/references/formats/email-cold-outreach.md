# Email Cold Outreach Format Playbook

Use this format for B2B cold outreach: sales, partnerships, customer development, founder-led outbound, expert interviews, and small-batch business outreach to people who did not subscribe to this specific sequence. Load `../channels/email.md` first.

Cold outreach is reusable channel/format guidance, not a Content Program by itself.

## Use Cases

- Ask for interest, permission to send a useful artifact, a referral to the right owner, a quick answer, a customer-development conversation, a partnership discussion, or a meeting when the value is already obvious.
- Create small-batch, relevance-driven outbound handoffs for a human sender.

## Required Inputs

- ICP, role, company type, exclusions, and list source.
- Trigger or reason now: hiring, launch, funding, technology, job post, public statement, workflow change, partnership fit, social/content signal, shared context, or role-based pain.
- Source freshness, verification status, risky/catch-all handling, and exclusion rules.
- Offer or insight exchanged for attention.
- Proof that can be stated truthfully: customer result, founder experience, benchmark, teardown, audit, integration, artifact, or credible observation.
- CTA preference, target geography, sender identity, suppression process, CRM/handoff destination, and reply owner.

If list source, jurisdiction, sender identity, suppression, opt-out handling, or consent basis is unclear, draft with a "not ready to send" risk note.

## Output Shape

- Segment and trigger summary.
- Compliance, permission, suppression, and deliverability notes.
- 3 to 6 subject options.
- First email body, usually 50 to 125 words excluding signature.
- Follow-up sequence with timing and new value per step.
- CTA rationale, personalization fields, exclusion/suppression notes, reply handling, manual sending notes, risks, and metrics.
- Campaign ledger fields for manual tracking: recipient/source, segment, trigger, sent step, reply status, suppression status, and CRM/handoff status.

Audience-facing copy must not include internal labels such as `segment:`, `CTA:`, or `personalization:`.

## Copy And Creative Rules

- List quality beats copy. If the segment has no shared pain or trigger, improve targeting before polishing prose.
- Signal quality beats volume. Recent, role-relevant signals are stronger than broad titles, stale databases, or cheap scraped lists.
- Use only good-enough verified contacts by default. Skip risky, catch-all, stale, role-inbox, or unverified addresses unless a human explicitly accepts the bounce and reputation risk.
- Define exclusions before drafting: current customers, open opportunities, competitors, partners, previous negative replies, unsubscribes, do-not-contact, recent bounces, and companies outside the ICP.
- First line should explain why this recipient or account, not flatter them generically.
- Body structure: relevance reason, problem hypothesis, value/proof, low-friction CTA, signature/opt-out where needed.
- Keep paragraphs short and mobile-readable.
- Use one CTA. Gong's findings support an interest-based CTA as a strong default; treat it as a bias toward permission, not a magic phrase.
- Subject lines should be plain, true, and classifying: topic, trigger, or specific noun phrase.
- Follow-ups must add a new angle, example, proof, or graceful close. Do not resend the same ask.
- Match sequence depth to relevance and market size. Broad or high-risk segments may need only one or two touches; narrow, high-fit segments can justify more only when every follow-up adds real value.
- Reverse lead magnets can work when they are real: offer a short teardown, audit, benchmark, example, checklist, or custom observation that the sender can actually provide. Do not imply manual effort or custom work if the artifact is generic or unavailable.
- Avoid spintax as a deliverability strategy. Use real segment, trigger, proof, and offer variation; keep grammar natural and avoid random synonyms inserted only to bypass filters.
- For cold email, minimize links, attachments, images, tracking pixels, and click-wrapped URLs. If a link is needed, use clear descriptive text and a trustworthy destination.

## Psychological Levers

- Fogg: make the desired reply easy, timely, and relevant.
- Reciprocity: offer a useful observation, teardown, benchmark, or example without creating obligation.
- Social proof: use peer-relevant proof only when verifiable and similar.
- Authority: cite expertise, customer work, or direct experience only when useful.
- Commitment: ask for a small reply before asking for a larger meeting.
- Reactance reduction: preserve autonomy with language such as "worth exploring?", "open to me sending it?", or "wrong person?"
- Reason-giving: answer why this person, why now, and why the sender.

Avoid fake scarcity, fake deadlines, guilt, shame, pressure, fake familiarity, fake `RE:`/`FWD:`, manufactured urgency, and tricks meant to disguise bulk sending.

## Platform Adaptation

- Founder-led: can use sharper personal observation and direct accountability.
- Sales outbound: emphasize role pain, proof, and clear next step.
- Partnership: emphasize mutual fit and specific collaboration path.
- Customer development: reduce selling and ask for a tiny insight or permission.
- PR/link outreach: show real familiarity with the recipient's work and avoid mass-template signals.

## Variants And Testing

- Test one variable at a time: segment, trigger, offer, proof, subject, first line, CTA, length, sequence depth, or follow-up angle.
- Use positive reply rate, qualified conversation rate, referral rate, opt-outs, complaints, bounces, and downstream pipeline. Opens are weak.
- Do not declare winners from tiny samples or from open rates alone.
- Track reply operations separately from copy: time to first meaningful response, percent of positive replies handled same day, CRM handoff completion, and missed-reply incidents.

## Review Checklist

- Recipient can tell why they were contacted.
- Segment fit and list source are credible or flagged.
- Contact freshness, verification status, risky-address handling, and exclusion criteria are explicit.
- Subject and body are truthful, concise, and non-deceptive.
- Personalization changes relevance rather than decorating a generic pitch.
- Any reverse lead magnet is genuinely available, useful, and not misrepresented as manual work when it is not.
- CTA is easy to answer from a phone.
- Opt-out, suppression, jurisdiction, and deliverability risks are addressed.
- A monitored owner can respond quickly to positive replies and route qualified conversations into the CRM or agreed handoff path.
- Follow-ups stop on opt-out, negative reply, hard bounce, wrong-person evidence, disqualifying signal, or completed CTA.

## Failure Diagnostics

- Delivery: authentication, blocks, spam placement, bounces, complaints, volume, and reputation.
- List: ICP fit, source quality, trigger accuracy, role accuracy, duplicates, stale contacts, catch-all/risky addresses, and missing exclusions.
- Offer: weak value, poor timing, unsupported proof, unclear differentiation.
- Copy: vague subject, fake personalization, long body, high-friction CTA, pressure tone.
- Follow-up: too many steps, no new value, poor stop rules, slow reply handling, weak CRM handoff.

Pause before writing more variants if bounces, complaints, angry replies, or opt-outs are elevated.

## Anti-Patterns

- Bought or scraped lists with no business reason review.
- "Loved your post" personalization followed by an unrelated pitch.
- Calendar links in first touch when no interest exists.
- Multiple asks in one email.
- "Just bumping this" follow-ups with no new value.
- Hidden opt-out or continued follow-up after negative response.
- Spintax or AI variation used mainly to disguise bulk sending.
- Bought, cheap, recycled, or unverified lists.
- Disposable domains, mailbox replacement, or domain cycling used to outrun reputation damage.
- Offering a custom audit, teardown, or benchmark that the sender cannot actually provide.

# One-Pager Format Playbook

Use this format for sales one-pagers, product sheets, partner briefs, event handouts, battlecards-lite, solution summaries, and leave-behinds. Load `../channels/events-sales.md` first.

## Source Guidance

- Gartner buyer and sales enablement context: https://www.gartner.com/en/sales/topics/sales-enablement
- FTC endorsement guidance for testimonials/proof: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides
- Google developer style guidance can help keep technical one-pagers clear: https://developers.google.com/style

## Use Cases

- Give a buyer, partner, analyst, attendee, customer, or internal team a compact artifact that summarizes value, fit, proof, and next steps.
- Support forwarding inside a buying committee.

## Required Inputs

- Audience, use case, distribution context, stage, product/solution, core problem, proof, CTA, and design constraints.
- Required sections, claims, customer names/logos, integrations, pricing/security constraints, and approval owner.

## Output Shape

- Headline, subhead, problem, solution, key benefits, how it works, proof, fit/not-fit, specs/integrations, CTA, and footnotes/claim notes.
- Visual hierarchy suggestions and manual design notes.

## Copy And Creative Rules

- Make it forwardable. A new reader should understand what it is without the sender explaining.
- Prioritize decision criteria over exhaustive features.
- Use bullets for scannability, but keep each bullet specific.
- Include proof close to value claims.
- Reserve fine print for necessary caveats, not hidden limitations.
- Keep the CTA concrete and contextual.

## Psychological Levers

- Fluency: a clean one-page structure reduces buyer effort.
- Risk reduction: fit, requirements, proof, and next step lower uncertainty.
- Authority: concise diagnosis and evidence beat dense claims.
- Social proof: use relevant customer proof only with approval.
- Commitment: one next step is stronger than many choices.

## Platform Adaptation

- Sales leave-behind: buyer problem, proof, objections, next step.
- Product sheet: capabilities, integrations, requirements, and setup.
- Partner one-pager: joint audience, value exchange, roles, and campaign idea.
- Event handout: session summary, resource links, CTA, and contact path.

## Variants And Testing

- Test headline, section order, proof type, CTA, and persona-specific versions.
- Track downloads, forwards, seller usage, meeting advancement, content-influenced pipeline, and buyer questions.
- Use questions from recipients to tighten future versions.

## Review Checklist

- The one-pager is useful without a live presenter.
- It answers who it is for, what problem it solves, why trust it, and what to do next.
- Claims, logos, stats, integrations, pricing, and security language are verified or flagged.
- Design constraints and manual approval steps are included.

## Failure Diagnostics

- Not forwarded: too generic, too long, or unclear audience.
- No conversion: CTA weak or buyer risk unanswered.
- Seller avoidance: artifact does not fit real conversation stages.
- Legal edits heavy: claims/proof not grounded early.

## Anti-Patterns

- Two-page copy squeezed into tiny text.
- Feature grids with no decision logic.
- Unsupported customer logos or metrics.
- Vague CTA such as "learn more" with no destination.
- Treating it like a brochure instead of a buying aid.

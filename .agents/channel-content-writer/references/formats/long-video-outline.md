# Long Video Outline Format Playbook

Use this format for YouTube long-form, product demos, explainers, educational videos, interviews, tutorials, and recorded thought-leadership video. Load `../channels/video-audio.md` and any specific channel guide first.

## Source Guidance

- YouTube title and thumbnail guidance: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12340300
- YouTube creator resources: https://www.youtube.com/creators/
- YouTube upload and optimization guidance: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/57407
- FTC endorsement guidance where sponsorships, affiliates, or partner mentions appear: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides

## Use Cases

- Deeply explain a topic, demonstrate a workflow, compare alternatives, document a customer story, teach a tutorial, or support sales/customer success.
- Turn a strategic idea into a searchable, watchable asset.
- Create a parent asset that can later produce clips, Shorts, blog posts, email snippets, and sales enablement material.

## Required Inputs

- Viewer intent, target knowledge level, video objective, expected length, and platform.
- Core promise, title/thumbnail direction, and proof sources.
- Product/demo assets, screenshots, B-roll, guest/talent availability, and claims to verify.
- CTA, destination links, disclosures, and production constraints.

## Output Shape

- Viewer promise and title/thumbnail options.
- Cold open or opening hook.
- Structured outline with sections, talking points, visuals, proof, examples, and transitions.
- Retention notes, B-roll/screen-recording needs, chapters, description, pinned comment, and CTA.
- Manual production, rights, approval, and analytics notes.

## Copy And Creative Rules

- Start by validating the click: say or show why the viewer is in the right place.
- Avoid long intros. Use the first minute to create confidence and momentum.
- Sequence from problem to stakes to method to example to outcome to next step.
- Add pattern breaks: visual changes, diagrams, demos, counterexamples, questions, and recaps.
- Write conversational talking points, not a dense essay.
- Include chapter labels that help viewers navigate and help search/comprehension.
- Make the CTA match viewer readiness: subscribe, watch next, download, book, trial, comment, or share.

## Psychological Levers

- Promise-payoff consistency builds trust.
- Open loops sustain attention when they are resolved clearly.
- Specific examples reduce cognitive load.
- Authority comes from proof and clarity, not credentials alone.
- Agency improves conversion: let viewers choose the next best step.

Avoid false suspense, exaggerated stakes, and bait-and-switch titles.

## Platform Adaptation

- YouTube: title/thumbnail/first minute are inseparable; include description, chapters, end screen, and comment strategy.
- Webinar replay: shorten intro and add chapterized on-demand notes.
- Sales/customer-success video: emphasize buyer questions, proof, objections, and follow-up assets.
- Product demo: show real workflow early and call out prerequisites.

## Variants And Testing

- Test title/thumbnail framing, opening structure, length, CTA, proof order, and demo depth.
- Track impressions, CTR, average view duration, retention curve, comments, subscribers gained, chapter drop-off, end-screen clicks, and conversion.
- Review comment language for future topics and objections.

## Review Checklist

- Title, thumbnail, opening, and content promise match.
- The outline contains visual moments, not only narration.
- Claims, screenshots, customer references, sponsorships, and rights are verified or flagged.
- Description, chapters, pinned comment, and CTA are included when relevant.
- Manual production and upload tasks are explicit.

## Failure Diagnostics

- Low CTR: title/thumbnail promise unclear, too broad, or not differentiated.
- Early drop-off: intro too slow or mismatch with title.
- Mid-video drop-off: weak structure, no pattern breaks, or too much abstraction.
- Low conversion: CTA late, irrelevant, or unsupported by video content.

## Anti-Patterns

- Starting with brand history before the viewer gets value.
- Writing an article and calling it a script.
- Overloading one video with multiple audiences.
- Misleading thumbnails.
- Ending without a concrete next step or viewer payoff.

# Event Hosting Format Playbook

Use this format when the brand owns the room: meetups, workshops, field events, customer dinners, breakfast briefings, office hours, partner events, community nights, launch events, and local technical sessions. Load `../channels/events-sales.md` first.

## Source Guidance

- Eventbrite event planning and strategy resources: https://www.eventbrite.com/blog/event-planning/ and https://www.eventbrite.com/blog/event-strategy/
- Cvent event planning, event marketing, venue sourcing, and budgeting resources: https://www.cvent.com/en/blog/events/event-planning-guide, https://www.cvent.com/en/blog/events/event-marketing-guide, and https://www.cvent.com/en/blog/events/event-budgeting-guide
- FTC endorsement guidance for sponsors, partners, or paid speakers: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides
- ON24 webinar resources when the hosted event is virtual or hybrid: https://www.on24.com/webinar-benchmarks/

## Use Cases

- Build a local community, teach a market, recruit users, create partner leverage, collect first-party questions, generate sales conversations, or turn expertise into reusable content.
- Host small events where the room quality matters more than raw registration count.
- Create a repeatable event operating system that can run four to six times per year without reinventing logistics.

## Required Inputs

- Event thesis: audience, why now, why this room, and what attendees should understand, decide, or be able to do afterward.
- Host posture: educational, community, demand generation, customer success, partner, recruiting, investor, launch, or retention.
- Capacity model: target attendance, registration cap, no-show assumption, waitlist rule, expected walk-ins, and minimum viable room size.
- Acquisition plan: invite list, owned channels, partner channels, speaker promotion, community posts, paid support if any, reminder cadence, and registration source tracking.
- Location plan: city, venue candidates, room layout, travel/parking/public transport, accessibility, insurance, contract owner, load-in time, closing time, and cancellation terms.
- Hospitality plan: coffee, water, soft drinks, alcoholic drinks, snacks, meal, dietary labels, glassware/cups, cleanup, and who restocks during the event.
- Production plan: AV, WiFi, adapters, microphones, clicker, recording/photo consent, signage, check-in, badges, speaker prep, demo fallback, staffing, and incident owner.
- Budget plan: venue, food, beverages, speaker travel, design/print, swag, photography, tools, contingency, sponsor reimbursement rules, receipt owner, and approval threshold.

## Output Shape

- Event one-pager: title, promise, audience, format, target date, city, capacity, budget, sponsor/partner notes, CTA, and success criteria.
- Timeline: eight-week or compressed plan covering venue, speaker, registration, promotion, reminders, assets, day-of operations, follow-up, and retro.
- Run of show: setup, doors, welcome, content, interaction, networking, closing, teardown, and owner for each beat.
- Logistics checklist: venue, AV, food, beverages, signage, badges, supplies, accessibility, safety, cleanup, receipts, and reimbursement.
- Promotion bundle: registration page copy, social posts, partner/speaker kit, email invite, reminder copy, and post-event recap prompt.
- Follow-up bundle: attendee email, no-show email, speaker thank-you, sponsor proof pack, sales/community routing, and next-event teaser.

## Copy And Creative Rules

- Lead with the attendee's reason to show up, not the host's reason to host. The event title should make the transformation or room obvious.
- Write registration copy like a promise with constraints: who it is for, what they will leave with, how interactive it is, and what is not covered.
- Keep partner and sponsor mentions useful. A logo can earn trust, but the content still needs a real point of view.
- Design the first ten minutes deliberately: signage, check-in, host greeting, drink access, room energy, and opening line set the trust level for the whole event.
- Put interaction before the final quarter. If the first audience action happens at Q&A, most people will stay passive.
- Treat food and beverage as part of the experience. Poor labeling, empty water, awkward alcohol pressure, or slow service will damage the perceived quality of the content.
- Create content capture moments before the event: one quote-worthy slide, one photo moment, one audience question, one short recap angle, and one follow-up resource.

## Psychological Levers

- Specificity reduces calendar friction. "A practical 90-minute workshop for operations leads building agentic workflows" beats "AI event."
- Social proof works best before registration through speaker credibility, partner brand, past attendee quotes, clear audience labels, and named use cases.
- Reciprocity starts with hospitality. Easy arrival, useful materials, and a respectful run of show make follow-up feel earned.
- Commitment increases attendance. Calendar holds, reminder value, pre-event question prompts, and a small pre-work request make the event feel real.
- Scarcity helps only when honest. Use capacity limits, waitlists, or curated roundtable seats when they are operationally true.

## Platform Adaptation

- Meetup or community night: lighter content, stronger host warmth, clear networking design, and a low-pressure next event invite.
- Workshop: prerequisites, exercise materials, facilitator count, table supplies, and a visible artifact attendees can complete.
- Executive breakfast/dinner: curated guest list, fewer slides, more moderated discussion, confidentiality norms, and direct follow-up.
- Partner event: co-branded promise, list-use rules, sponsor recognition, speaker balance, reimbursement/approval path, and proof-of-delivery pack.
- Hybrid event: one audience is always at risk of becoming second-class. Assign a remote moderator, camera/mic plan, and interaction path.

## Variants And Testing

- Test title, audience label, registration promise, speaker mix, venue, day/time, reminder timing, CTA, and content-to-networking ratio.
- Track source-level registration, attendance rate, no-show rate, attendee quality, questions, meetings booked, opt-ins, survey score, community joins, content reuse, and reimbursement status.
- Run a post-event retro within 48 hours while memory is fresh: what filled the room, what confused people, what broke operationally, what questions repeated, and what should become content.
- Keep a repeatable vendor and venue log: response speed, cost, AV quality, food quality, staff helpfulness, accessibility, and attendee comments.

## Review Checklist

- Event promise, audience, format, and success criteria are clear.
- Host versus partner/sponsor responsibilities are separated.
- Budget includes contingency and receipt/reimbursement ownership.
- Venue, AV, WiFi, accessibility, food, beverages, signage, staffing, and cleanup are assigned.
- Registration, reminders, waitlist, and no-show assumptions are defined.
- Sponsor mentions, recording/photo consent, and data-use rules are safe.
- Follow-up emails, sales/community routing, recap content, and retro owner are planned before the event.

## Failure Diagnostics

- Low registration: weak audience label, vague title, poor channel fit, insufficient partner promotion, or topic too broad.
- High no-show rate: weak reminder value, no pre-event commitment, inconvenient timing, free event with no perceived cost, or poor calendar copy.
- Awkward room: unclear host role, no arrival design, poor seating, no early interaction, or content/networking ratio mismatch.
- Budget surprise: missing beverage assumptions, overtime, AV rental, print/signage, speaker travel, taxes/service fees, or cleanup charges.
- Weak follow-up: no segmentation, no owner, no notes, no CTA matched to attendee intent, or delay longer than 48 hours.

## Anti-Patterns

- Treating the event as a product pitch with snacks.
- Choosing a venue before defining the room behavior.
- Assuming free means easy to fill.
- Ignoring beverages, dietary needs, signage, check-in, receipts, or cleanup until event day.
- Letting sponsors dominate the attendee promise.
- Running Q&A as the only interaction.
- Ending with "we will follow up soon" without a prepared follow-up path.

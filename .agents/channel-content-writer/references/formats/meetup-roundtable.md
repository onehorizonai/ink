# Meetup And Roundtable Format Playbook

Use this format for local meetups, community sessions, founder/operator roundtables, user groups, peer dinners, breakfast briefings, invite-only salons, and small learning circles. Load `../channels/events-sales.md`, `event-hosting.md`, and `event-promotion-follow-up.md` first.

## Source Guidance

- Eventbrite event planning and strategy resources: https://www.eventbrite.com/blog/event-planning/ and https://www.eventbrite.com/blog/event-strategy/
- Cvent event planning and budgeting resources: https://www.cvent.com/en/blog/events/event-planning-guide and https://www.cvent.com/en/blog/events/event-budgeting-guide
- FTC endorsement guidance for sponsored or partner-supported events: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides

## Use Cases

- Build a trusted local or niche community through repeatable rooms.
- Host a small curated discussion where the relationship quality is the product.
- Turn a broad category like AI, fintech, developer tools, or operations into practical peer learning.
- Use a partner/sponsor brand to reduce friction while keeping the event useful and non-salesy.

## Required Inputs

- Room type: open meetup, curated roundtable, invite-only dinner, user group, breakfast briefing, fireside chat, lightning-talk night, or networking drinks.
- Audience: role, company size, technical level, seniority, geography, community maturity, and whether competitors can attend.
- Curation rules: open RSVP, approval required, invite-only, waitlist, target mix, maximum per company, and who gets declined.
- Conversation design: talk length, prompt, moderator questions, table groups, introductions, participation norms, and confidentiality.
- Hospitality: venue, seating, arrival drink, non-alcoholic options, snacks/meal, dietary needs, name badges, coat storage, noise level, and closing flow.
- Follow-up: attendee list rules, intro facilitation, recap level, private notes, community invite, next event, and sales boundary.

## Output Shape

- Event brief: audience, room type, promise, curation rules, format, capacity, venue, partner/sponsor note, and success criteria.
- Facilitation plan: welcome script, intro format, discussion prompts, time boxes, moderator interventions, close, and networking bridge.
- Invitation copy: public page or private invite, speaker/partner copy, reminders, and expected participation.
- Logistics plan: seating, food/beverage, signage, check-in, badges, supplies, photography consent, and host roles.
- Follow-up pack: attendee thank-you, no-show note, intro request process, recap angle, community invite, and next-event teaser.

## Copy And Creative Rules

- For meetups, lead with useful belonging: who should be in the room and what conversation they will be part of.
- For roundtables, lead with curation and confidentiality. The value is the quality of peers, not the stage content.
- Keep the sponsor note understated and clear. Thank sponsors, explain why their support helps, and avoid turning the room into an ad.
- Write prompts that produce stories, tradeoffs, and examples. Avoid prompts that only produce opinions.
- Make networking less awkward: arrival host, badge clarity, starter prompts, table assignments when helpful, and a closing bridge.
- Protect discussion quality. A few strong attendees are better than a room full of mismatched registrations.

## Psychological Levers

- Belonging drives repeat attendance. People return when they can predict the kind of person and conversation in the room.
- Safety creates candor. Clear confidentiality norms, no hard selling, and moderator control make people share real problems.
- Status can help curation, but overdoing exclusivity makes the room brittle.
- Reciprocity works through introductions, useful notes, and thoughtful follow-up.
- Local identity matters. Use city, ecosystem, and venue cues to make the event feel anchored rather than generic.

## Platform Adaptation

- Open meetup: broader promotion, lower barrier, stronger host warmth, simple registration, and visible first-timer welcome.
- Curated roundtable: approval process, smaller capacity, richer pre-event questions, and stronger confidentiality.
- Dinner or breakfast: fewer slides, more prompts, seating plan, dietary detail, and conversation pacing around service.
- User group: product/community education, user stories, feedback loop, support notes, and community continuity.
- Partner community event: sponsor/partner thanks, co-promotion, audience ownership rules, and shared proof pack.

## Variants And Testing

- Test topic specificity, audience label, capacity, curation method, day/time, venue, food/beverage level, talk-to-networking ratio, and follow-up style.
- Track registrations, acceptance rate, attendance, repeat attendance, first-timer ratio, introductions made, questions asked, survey comments, community joins, and next-event demand.
- Maintain a community map: regulars, connectors, speakers, sponsors, venues, partners, and people to invite back.
- Review attendee mix after every event. Many community events fail slowly through audience drift.

## Review Checklist

- Room type, curation rule, and audience mix are clear.
- Topic and prompts invite concrete examples, not generic opinions.
- Sponsor/partner role is useful and disclosed.
- Venue, seating, food, beverages, badges, and arrival flow support conversation.
- Host, moderator, and networking roles are assigned.
- Follow-up respects privacy and does not over-sell.
- Next-event path is ready before the current event ends.

## Failure Diagnostics

- Low registration: topic too generic, community not defined, weak partner/speaker pull, or invite copy lacks specificity.
- Wrong room: curation too loose, channel mismatch, unclear seniority/role, or registrations optimized for volume.
- Quiet discussion: weak prompts, no early participation, unsafe sales energy, poor moderator intervention, or seating mismatch.
- Poor repeat attendance: no continuity, weak follow-up, no community identity, or events feel random.
- Sponsor tension: sponsor expectations not aligned with attendee value.

## Anti-Patterns

- "Networking drinks" with no reason for this group to gather.
- Roundtables with too many people.
- Letting one loud guest own the room.
- Using community trust as a lead-gen trap.
- Over-branding a local event until it stops feeling local.
- Capturing private discussion notes as public content without permission.

# Event Promotion And Follow-Up Format Playbook

Use this format for event landing pages, registration copy, invite emails, reminder emails, speaker kits, partner kits, social posts, community posts, recap content, replay copy, attendee follow-up, no-show follow-up, sales handoff, and next-event promotion.

## Source Guidance

- Eventbrite event marketing and strategy resources: https://www.eventbrite.com/blog/event-strategy/
- Cvent event marketing guide: https://www.cvent.com/en/blog/events/event-marketing-guide
- ON24 webinar benchmark and engagement resources for virtual follow-up: https://www.on24.com/webinar-benchmarks/
- FTC endorsement guidance for sponsor and partner disclosure: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides

## Use Cases

- Fill a room with the right people, reduce no-shows, increase live engagement, and make follow-up useful.
- Give speakers, sponsors, partners, and team members copy they can actually share.
- Convert event questions into content, sales notes, community momentum, and a stronger next event.

## Required Inputs

- Event promise, audience, date/time, city/platform, venue, capacity, price/free status, speakers, sponsor/partner notes, agenda, CTA, and registration URL.
- Audience segments: prospects, customers, partners, community members, speakers' audiences, local ecosystem, press/media, employees, no-shows, attendees, high-engagement attendees, and VIPs.
- Acquisition channels: email, LinkedIn, X, communities, partner newsletters, speaker posts, website banner, in-product message, sales outreach, paid promotion, and local ecosystem groups.
- Reminder plan: confirmation, calendar hold, one-week reminder, one-day reminder, same-day reminder, travel/parking note, pre-work, and post-event replay/resource timing.
- Follow-up routing: attendee tags, question tags, demo interest, partner interest, community invite, sales owner, support owner, and content owner.

## Output Shape

- Registration page copy: title, subtitle, audience, outcomes, agenda, speaker/host section, venue/platform details, sponsor note, accessibility note, and CTA.
- Promotion kit: social posts, email invite, partner/speaker blurbs, short DM invite, community post, image brief, and UTM/source notes.
- Reminder sequence: confirmation, value reminder, logistics reminder, final reminder, and calendar copy.
- Live capture prompts: questions to log, quotes to request permission for, photo/video moments, poll prompts, and content themes.
- Follow-up sequence: attendee email, no-show email, VIP note, speaker thank-you, sponsor proof pack, sales/community handoff, recap post, and next-event teaser.

## Copy And Creative Rules

- The title should name either the outcome, the room, or the tension. Avoid generic "AI meetup" style titles unless the brand/community already carries the demand.
- Registration copy must answer: who is this for, why now, what will happen, what will I leave with, who is in the room, and what is the commitment?
- Use reminders to add value, not just say "do not forget." Add parking, prep questions, speaker preview, worksheet, or what to bring.
- Speaker/partner kits should be copy-pasteable but not robotic. Include one short post, one longer post, one email blurb, and one image/asset note.
- Follow-up should be segmented. Attendees, no-shows, question askers, VIPs, and high-intent leads should not get the same note.
- Recap content should share the useful insight, not just prove the event happened.

## Psychological Levers

- Clarity lowers registration friction. Specific audience and outcomes beat broad promise.
- Calendar commitment increases attendance. Confirmation copy should make the event feel worth protecting.
- Repeated value beats repeated reminders. Every touch should make attendance feel smarter.
- Identity drives community events. People show up when they recognize "people like me are in that room."
- Fast follow-up preserves emotional context. Send the first useful follow-up within 24 to 48 hours.

## Platform Adaptation

- Email: segment by relationship and intent; use plain language, clear logistics, and calendar links.
- LinkedIn: lead with professional relevance, speaker credibility, local ecosystem value, or practical artifact.
- Communities: respect group rules, avoid repeated promotion, and frame the event as useful to the members.
- Sales outreach: use a low-pressure invitation with a reason tied to the account/person, not a disguised meeting request.
- Webinar replay: use chapters, resource links, and behavior-based follow-up.

## Variants And Testing

- Test title, subtitle, audience label, speaker order, proof, CTA, reminder timing, partner kit, and follow-up segmentation.
- Track registration source, landing-page conversion, email open/click, social saves/shares/comments, partner contribution, attendance rate, no-show rate, reply rate, meetings booked, replay views, and next-event signups.
- Keep a swipe file of top-performing titles, reminders, follow-ups, and attendee questions.
- Compare channels by attendee quality, not just registrations.

## Review Checklist

- Registration page promise is specific and matched to the actual event.
- Promotion assets exist for owned, partner, speaker, and community channels.
- Reminder sequence includes logistics and value, not only urgency.
- Sponsor/partner references are accurate and approved.
- Capture prompts and permission boundaries are ready.
- Follow-up is segmented by attendance and intent.
- Sales/community/content owners know what they receive after the event.

## Failure Diagnostics

- Registration page converts poorly: title too vague, audience too broad, value unclear, agenda missing, speaker credibility hidden, or CTA buried.
- Registrations do not attend: reminder sequence weak, commitment low, timing poor, or event value not reinforced.
- Wrong people attend: acquisition channels or audience label mismatched.
- Follow-up underperforms: same message sent to all, no conversation context, delayed send, weak CTA, or no useful resource.
- No content lift: capture plan missing, photos unusable, questions not logged, or recap angle chosen after memory faded.

## Anti-Patterns

- "Join us for an evening of networking and insights" without a sharper promise.
- Posting the same promo copy everywhere.
- Treating reminders as nagging instead of trust-building.
- Sending the same follow-up to attendees and no-shows.
- Recaps that only thank everyone and say the event was great.
- Forgetting partner/speaker share copy until launch day.

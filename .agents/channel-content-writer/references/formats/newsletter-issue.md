# Newsletter Issue Format Playbook

Use this format for subscribed editorial or marketing newsletters. Load `../channels/owned-email.md` and `../channels/email.md` first.

## Source Guidance

- Mailchimp subject-line, preview-text, and deliverability guidance: https://mailchimp.com/help/best-practices-for-email-subject-lines/, https://mailchimp.com/help/about-preview-text/, and https://mailchimp.com/resources/email-content-for-deliverability/
- Google, Yahoo, and Microsoft sender guidance: https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126, https://senders.yahooinc.com/best-practices/, and https://sendersupport.olc.protection.outlook.com/pm/policies
- FTC CAN-SPAM guide: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business
- Apple Mail Privacy Protection: https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/data/en/mail-privacy-protection/

## Use Cases

- Send a recurring editorial issue, product digest, founder note, curated links, event/update roundup, or community letter to people who expect it.
- Build trust and habit while creating a path to replies, clicks, activation, sales, or retention.

## Required Inputs

- List promise, subscriber segment, permission state, cadence, sender identity, and issue goal.
- Primary story or theme, supporting links, proof, product/customer claims, and CTA.
- Desired tone, house sections, sponsorships, disclosures, and suppression requirements.

## Output Shape

- Subject options and preview text options.
- Issue structure with sections, body copy, links, CTA, and optional sponsor/disclosure blocks.
- Plain-text or accessibility notes when relevant.
- Manual send QA: list, suppression, test send, links, rendering, approvals, and metrics.

## Copy And Creative Rules

- Reward the open quickly. The opening should prove the issue is worth the subscriber's attention.
- Pass a send-worthiness gate before drafting: this issue should help the subscriber learn, decide, act, adopt, attend, or feel meaningfully included. Do not send every blog post, vanity milestone, internal update, or low-context announcement by default.
- Use one dominant idea. Roundups can include multiple links, but still need an editorial reason for existing.
- Make preview text a second hook, not a subject repeat.
- Keep sections scannable: short headers, clear summaries, and obvious links.
- Link text should describe the destination. Avoid "click here".
- Use promotional blocks sparingly and make them clearly relevant to the list promise.
- Do not write subject lines that overpromise or hide commercial intent.

## Psychological Levers

- Habit: consistent sections help subscribers know what they will get.
- Reciprocity: useful curation or insight earns clicks and replies.
- Familiarity: sender voice and cadence build trust over time.
- Curiosity: use it to open a useful issue, not to hide a weak one.
- Autonomy: give subscribers meaningful choices such as read, save, reply, or visit.

## Platform Adaptation

- Editorial newsletter: lead with insight and reader value.
- Product newsletter: lead with what changed, who it helps, and why now.
- Community newsletter: highlight members, questions, events, and contribution paths.
- Sponsor-supported newsletter: make sponsorships clearly disclosed and contextually useful.

## Variants And Testing

- Test subject, preview text, lead story, CTA, section order, sender name, and issue length.
- Measure clicks, replies, conversions, unsubscribes, complaints, bounces, subscriber growth, and downstream behavior.
- Use opens only as directional because privacy protections can distort them.

## Review Checklist

- Subscriber expectation and permission state are clear.
- The issue has a recipient benefit strong enough to justify an inbox interruption.
- Subject and preview text are truthful and non-duplicative.
- The issue has one editorial center of gravity.
- Links, claims, sponsor notes, unsubscribe, and compliance elements are verified or flagged.
- Manual ESP setup, rendering, and analytics tasks are separate from copy.

## Failure Diagnostics

- Low clicks: issue lacks a clear payoff, links are buried, or CTAs are mismatched.
- High unsubscribes: content drifted from list promise or cadence is too aggressive.
- Low replies: issue does not invite a specific human response.
- Deliverability issues: check authentication, complaints, bounces, list age, link/image weight, and sender reputation.

## Anti-Patterns

- Turning every issue into a sales blast.
- Sending internal milestones, new blog posts, or generic announcements when they do not give the subscriber a clear benefit.
- Repeating the subject line in preview text.
- Hiding the best idea below long housekeeping.
- Stuffing too many CTAs into one issue.
- Publishing without link, rendering, and suppression QA.

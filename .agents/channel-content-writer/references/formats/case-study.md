# Case Study Format Playbook

Use this format for customer stories, proof narratives, sales case studies, mini case studies, partner stories, and results writeups. Load `../channels/events-sales.md` first.

## Source Guidance

- Scale Venture Partners guidance on B2B case studies: https://www.scalevp.com/resources/building-a-great-b2b-case-study
- Arcade case-study writing guidance and examples: https://www.arcade.software/post/how-to-write-a-case-study
- Gartner sales enablement context for buyer confidence: https://www.gartner.com/en/sales/topics/sales-enablement
- FTC endorsement guidance for testimonials and typicality: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides

## Use Cases

- Prove value to buyers, equip sellers, support a launch, document customer success, address objections, or show a before/after transformation.

## Required Inputs

- Customer approval status, customer profile, problem, stakes, alternatives, solution, implementation, results, timeline, quotes, and proof.
- What can be named publicly, anonymized, or not used.
- Target buyer persona, sales objection, CTA, and approval process.

## Output Shape

- Title, summary, customer context, challenge, solution, implementation, results, quotes, lessons, CTA, and claim/approval notes.
- Optional short version, sales snippet, social snippets, and one-slide proof.

## Copy And Creative Rules

- Lead with the business problem and result, not the vendor.
- Make the customer the protagonist.
- Use specific numbers only when verified and approved. Explain measurement context.
- Show the messy middle: why the problem mattered, what changed, and what made the result possible.
- Include credible limitations or context when needed.
- Avoid turning the story into a product feature list.
- Pull quotes should sound like a real customer, not polished marketing slogans.

## Psychological Levers

- Social proof works when the customer resembles the target buyer.
- Narrative makes proof memorable.
- Risk reduction comes from implementation detail and lessons learned.
- Authority grows through evidence and specificity.
- Identification: buyers should see their own problem in the story.

## Platform Adaptation

- Full case study: narrative depth, quotes, metrics, and implementation.
- Mini case study: problem, action, result, one quote, next step.
- Sales proof: objection-specific excerpt, metric, and customer fit.
- Anonymous case study: explain why anonymized and use enough context to remain credible.

## Variants And Testing

- Test title/result framing, persona-specific intros, metric-first versus story-first, CTA, and excerpt formats.
- Track sales usage, influenced pipeline, conversion from proof pages, content engagement, stakeholder forwards, and objection reduction.
- Collect seller/customer-success feedback on what buyers ask after reading.

## Review Checklist

- Customer approval, quote approval, logo/name rights, and metric approval are explicit.
- Results are specific, typicality/caveats are clear, and claims are supported.
- Story centers the customer and buyer problem.
- CTA and sales-use notes are included.
- Manual legal/customer review is listed.

## Failure Diagnostics

- Weak credibility: no numbers, quotes, context, or customer specificity.
- Weak sales impact: story does not map to a buyer objection or segment.
- Approval delays: claims, quotes, or confidential details not separated early.
- Low engagement: title lacks outcome or story opens too slowly.

## Anti-Patterns

- Vendor-as-hero storytelling.
- Unsupported ROI or vague "saved time" claims.
- Fake-sounding quotes.
- Hiding implementation effort.
- Publishing without customer/legal approval.

# Livestream And Webinar Format Playbook

Use this format for webinars, livestreams, virtual events, live demos, fireside chats, office hours, and recorded sessions. Load `../channels/video-audio.md` and any event/channel guide first.

## Source Guidance

- ON24 webinar benchmarks and digital engagement resources: https://www.on24.com/webinar-benchmarks/ and https://www.on24.com/resources/asset/digital-engagement-benchmarks-report/
- ON24 benchmark-report methodology for customer comparisons: https://support.on24.com/hc/en-us/articles/21420783063579-Webinar-Benchmark-Reports
- YouTube live/creator resources when streaming to YouTube: https://www.youtube.com/creators/
- FTC endorsement guidance for sponsors, partners, or paid speakers: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides

## Use Cases

- Teach a complex topic, generate pipeline, launch a product, host a customer story, support community, train users, or collect high-intent questions.
- Combine presentation, demo, Q&A, polls, chat, offers, and follow-up into one event experience.

## Required Inputs

- Audience, event goal, registration promise, speakers, date/time, runtime, platform, and moderation owner.
- Event type: educational, demand-gen, product demo, training, partner, community, analyst/customer, or office hours.
- Offer/CTA, follow-up plan, sponsor/partner disclosures, and data/privacy constraints.
- Assets: slides, demo, polls, handouts, landing page, reminder emails, and replay plan.

## Output Shape

- Event positioning: title, abstract, audience, outcomes, and registration copy.
- Run of show with timings, speaker roles, interaction moments, transitions, and contingency notes.
- Slide or section outline, talk track, demo notes, poll/Q&A prompts, chat prompts, and closing CTA.
- Follow-up email/replay notes, repurposing ideas, and manual production checklist.

## Copy And Creative Rules

- The registration promise must name what attendees will learn or be able to do.
- Open live sessions fast: welcome, agenda, why it matters, how to participate, and first value.
- Plan interaction before drafting slides. Polls, chat, Q&A, worksheets, and demos should serve the learning journey.
- Use fewer, stronger slides. Webinars fail when they become narrated PDFs.
- Make the CTA contextual: book a consult, try a workflow, download template, join community, watch replay, or answer a question.
- Write a replay/on-demand version with a shorter intro and chapter-like structure.

## Psychological Levers

- Time investment needs a clear payoff.
- Participation increases commitment when polls/questions are genuinely useful.
- Authority is strongest through demonstration, customer proof, and precise answers.
- Social proof can come from peer questions and live examples, but never fabricate attendee momentum.
- Autonomy matters: offer multiple next steps for mixed-intent attendees.

## Platform Adaptation

- Webinar platforms: optimize for registration, attendance, engagement score, Q&A, poll participation, and follow-up.
- YouTube/LinkedIn livestreams: stronger public hook, chat moderation, thumbnail/title, and replay packaging.
- Customer training: reduce promotion, increase prerequisites, steps, and support links.
- Partner webinars: clarify speaker roles, audience ownership, list-use rules, and co-branding.

## Variants And Testing

- Test event title, abstract, speaker mix, registration page, reminder cadence, opening structure, poll placement, and CTA.
- Track registration rate, attendance rate, watch time, engagement, Q&A volume, poll participation, CTA clicks, meetings, replay views, and pipeline/activation.
- Review questions asked live to improve future content and sales/support assets.

## Review Checklist

- The audience and promised outcome are specific.
- Run of show includes timing, interaction, moderation, and contingency notes.
- Claims, customer names, partner disclosures, and data/privacy uses are verified or flagged.
- Follow-up and replay handoff are included.
- Manual hosting, production, recording, and analytics tasks are explicit.

## Failure Diagnostics

- Low registration: title/abstract vague, audience too broad, offer weak, or promotion mismatch.
- Low attendance: reminder/value unclear, timing poor, or registration too casual.
- Low engagement: no planned interaction or content too lecture-heavy.
- Low conversion: CTA disconnected from session value or no follow-up path.

## Anti-Patterns

- Webinars that are sales decks with Q&A at the end.
- Long housekeeping before value.
- Polls with no purpose.
- Overloaded panels with no clear host.
- No replay, follow-up, or repurposing plan.

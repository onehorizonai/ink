# Event Attendance Format Playbook

Use this format when the team attends, sponsors, speaks at, exhibits at, or networks around an event it does not fully own. Load `../channels/events-sales.md` first.

## Source Guidance

- Cvent event marketing and venue/event management resources: https://www.cvent.com/en/blog/events/event-marketing-guide
- Gartner sales enablement overview for routing event conversations into buyer support: https://www.gartner.com/en/sales/topics/sales-enablement
- FTC endorsement guidance for sponsorship disclosures: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides

## Use Cases

- Decide whether an external event is worth attending, sponsoring, speaking at, or exhibiting at.
- Turn a conference or community event into meetings, partner relationships, content, market research, recruiting, or pipeline.
- Prepare a field plan so event work does not disappear into scattered notes and delayed follow-up.

## Required Inputs

- Event facts: name, date, location, audience, size, agenda, sponsor tiers, speaker rules, booth rules, attendee list access, recording rules, and organizer constraints.
- Strategic reason: target accounts, partner development, founder visibility, customer learning, recruiting, media/content, community trust, or competitive research.
- Attendance mode: attendee-only, speaker, panelist, sponsor, booth, side-event host, workshop host, partner guest, or customer meeting hub.
- Target list: named accounts, people, partners, journalists, creators, investors, community leaders, customers, or candidates.
- Meeting plan: outreach copy, ask, calendar slots, meeting location, backup meeting spots, qualification tags, and post-meeting owner.
- Capture plan: field notes, photos, short videos, session takeaways, voice memos, customer quotes, permission boundaries, and live social rules.
- Follow-up plan: SLA, CRM/list routing, owner, message variants, community invite, recap content, and next action.

## Output Shape

- Event selection memo: why this event, expected upside, cost, opportunity cost, risks, and go/no-go recommendation.
- Field plan: team roles, travel, agenda, target list, meeting slots, booth/sponsor obligations, content capture, and daily debrief.
- Messaging kit: pre-event outreach, social posts, booth or hallway pitch, speaker/session promo, and follow-up messages.
- Qualification model: conversation types, tags, priority levels, lead routing, disqualification rules, and next-step menu.
- Retro: meetings completed, useful intros, questions heard, competitor notes, content captured, follow-up completion, and whether to return next year.

## Copy And Creative Rules

- The pre-event message should give the recipient a reason to meet that is specific to the event context. Generic "are you attending?" copy is weak.
- Prepare one sentence for why you are there, one sentence for who you help, and one useful question that starts real conversation.
- Booth or hallway language must be scannable in seconds. Replace category jargon with the pain or outcome attendees recognize.
- Live content should report useful observations, not self-congratulation. Share patterns, questions, and takeaways without violating private conversations.
- Post-event follow-up should reference the actual conversation, session, or shared context. If the note could be sent to everyone, it is not a good follow-up.

## Psychological Levers

- Proximity creates momentum only when the ask is easy. Offer short windows, clear meeting points, and a specific reason to talk.
- Reciprocity works through useful preparation: intros, summaries, notes, resources, or an invite that helps the other person.
- Social proof at events is visible through who you meet, where you speak, and what other attendees ask, but never imply partnerships or customers that do not exist.
- Scarcity is natural in a conference schedule. Use limited slots truthfully and avoid pressure tactics.
- Memory decays fast after events. Same-day notes and next-day follow-up outperform polished messages sent a week later.

## Platform Adaptation

- Attendee-only: optimize for target meetings, session learning, hallway conversations, and content capture.
- Speaker: add talk promotion, speaker prep, audience capture, Q&A logging, and post-talk resource CTA.
- Sponsor: map deliverables, disclosure requirements, badge scanning rules, booth traffic plan, and proof-of-delivery assets.
- Booth: prepare staffing schedule, demo loop, qualification script, lead capture tags, swag policy, and escalation path for high-value conversations.
- Side event: use `event-hosting.md` for the hosted component and keep organizer rules for unofficial events explicit.

## Variants And Testing

- Test attendance mode, sponsor tier, target list quality, meeting ask, outreach timing, booth message, giveaway, demo format, and follow-up SLA.
- Track meetings requested, meetings booked, meetings completed, target accounts reached, qualified conversations, partner intros, badge scans, speaking attendance, content captured, follow-up completion, and sourced/influenced opportunities.
- Compare events by cost per useful conversation, not only cost per lead.
- Keep a return/no-return scorecard with audience fit, organizer quality, sponsor value, operational effort, and post-event outcomes.

## Review Checklist

- Event reason and success criteria are explicit.
- Target list and outreach plan are ready before travel.
- Team roles, schedule, meeting locations, and fallback plans are clear.
- Sponsor/booth/speaker obligations and restrictions are listed.
- Capture permissions and private-conversation boundaries are clear.
- Lead routing, follow-up copy, and owners are ready before the event starts.
- Retro fields are defined so the team can decide whether to repeat the event.

## Failure Diagnostics

- Low meeting count: target list built too late, ask too vague, no named reason to meet, or poor event-audience fit.
- Weak booth performance: unclear signage, passive staffing, no qualification path, demo too long, or no follow-up tags.
- Poor content yield: no capture owner, no permission rules, no field-note prompts, or sessions chosen without content goals.
- Slow follow-up: notes scattered, owner missing, CRM/list routing undefined, or team too tired after travel with no protected follow-up block.
- Bad ROI read: judged by badge scans instead of useful conversations, next steps, partner value, or learning.

## Anti-Patterns

- Buying sponsorship as a substitute for having a reason to be there.
- Showing up with no target list.
- Treating every badge scan as intent.
- Staffing a booth with people who cannot qualify or route conversations.
- Publishing private hallway conversations as content.
- Waiting a week to follow up.

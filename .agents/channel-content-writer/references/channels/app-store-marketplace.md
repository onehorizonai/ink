# App Store And Marketplace Channel Guidance

Use this guide for `app-store-listing` and `marketplace-listing` after loading `direct-product.md`. Pair it with `../formats/app-store-marketplace-listing.md`.

## Source Guidance

- Apple App Store product page guidance: https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page/
- Google Play store-listing best practices and policy guidance: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13393723
- Google Play Developer Policy Center: https://play.google.com/about/developer-content-policy/
- FTC endorsement guidance for reviews, ratings, testimonials, affiliate, and partner claims: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides

## Native Behavior

- Store and marketplace listings are decision pages at the moment of evaluation. Users compare name, icon, rating, screenshots, short description, first lines, privacy cues, compatibility, and reviews quickly.
- Apple and Google use metadata, screenshots, previews, categories, ratings, localization, and policy compliance differently. Treat platform as an input before writing.
- Screenshots and preview assets often do more conversion work than long descriptions. Copy must match the visual order.
- Search intent matters. Keywords should appear naturally in names, subtitles, short descriptions, and body copy where allowed, but keyword stuffing can harm trust and policy compliance.
- Marketplace buyers often need integration, permissions, setup, support, pricing, and security reassurance before installing.

## Audience And Context Rules

- Identify platform, category, buyer/user, use case, maturity, pricing model, competitor alternatives, and review objections.
- Separate app-store users from admins or marketplace buyers. An end user wants outcome and ease; an admin wants trust, compatibility, permissions, and support.
- Clarify the primary job: install, start trial, request access, add integration, buy template, compare vendor, or update existing users.
- Use the user's language, not internal feature names. Translate features into setup, workflow, relief, or result.
- Localize only when the product, screenshots, support, and claims can sustain the locale.

## Creative Strategy

- Lead with what the product does and who it helps. Do not open with brand philosophy.
- Make the first screenshot or gallery asset answer the highest-intent question. Use the next assets to show workflow, proof, differentiator, and trust.
- Use bullets for decision criteria: use cases, key features, integrations, privacy/security, support, and requirements.
- For Apple, account for how screenshots and previews appear in search/product contexts. For Google Play, treat the short description and feature graphic as high-impact assets.
- For B2B marketplaces, include installation path, permissions, supported plans, docs/support links, and proof.
- Draft release-note or "what's new" copy that is user-facing, not a commit log.

## Compliance And Risk

- Keep metadata accurate: product name, category, functionality, screenshots, preview videos, privacy statements, compatibility, pricing, and availability.
- Do not use competitor logos, platform marks, review quotes, rankings, awards, or customer names unless rights and policy allow them.
- Avoid deceptive behavior, impersonation, restricted content, unsupported medical/financial/security claims, and fake reviews.
- Flag screenshots containing personal data, production customer data, private keys, internal dashboards, or unreleased features.
- For marketplaces, verify integration permissions, security claims, procurement terms, and support commitments.

## Manual Boundary

Ink can draft listing copy, metadata options, screenshot captions, preview-video scripts, release notes, marketplace FAQs, ASO notes, and policy QA. A human must upload assets, configure listings, localize, submit for review, respond to rejections, manage ratings/reviews, and collect conversion data unless a real tool is used.

## Metrics

- App stores: impressions, product-page views, conversion rate, install rate, keyword ranking, ratings/reviews, uninstall rate, trial activation, and crash/support signals after install.
- Marketplaces: listing views, installs, trial starts, qualified leads, activation, integration completion, support tickets, reviews, and revenue.
- Diagnostic: screenshot order, short description, title/subtitle, icon, pricing clarity, review themes, localization, and traffic source.

## QA Checklist

- Platform, category, audience, and primary install/buying job are explicit.
- Copy and screenshots describe real product behavior.
- Keywords are natural and policy-safe.
- Claims, rights, privacy, pricing, compatibility, and review/testimonial use are verified or flagged.
- Manual submission, localization, asset, and analytics tasks are listed.

## Anti-Patterns

- Keyword stuffing in names or descriptions.
- Pretty screenshots that hide the actual app or workflow.
- Feature lists with no user decision logic.
- Inflated claims such as "best", "secure", "compliant", or "guaranteed" without support.
- Copy that sounds like a website hero instead of a store listing.

## Native Behavior

- Video and audio succeed through retention. The opening must make the viewer or listener understand why to continue before they drift.
- Short video is judged in seconds; long video earns attention through title/thumbnail promise, opening payoff, and segment pacing; audio earns attention through premise, host trust, and listening rhythm.
- Most short-form video is mobile-first and often sound-optional. Most webinars and podcasts require a stronger pre-event or pre-listen promise because the time commitment is larger.
- Production artifacts matter: hook, script, shot list, on-screen text, title, thumbnail, description, transcript, chapters, show notes, and follow-up assets.

## Audience And Context Rules

- Choose the attention job: entertain, teach, demonstrate, compare, announce, convert, deepen trust, or capture questions.
- Identify viewer/listener knowledge level and time tolerance before choosing length or depth.
- Make the content self-contained. Do not rely on a caption or show note to explain what the video should have made clear.
- Use examples, product views, visuals, or story beats where text-only explanation would drag.
- For live formats, plan interaction before writing the content: Q&A, chat, poll, hand raise, worksheet, breakout, or follow-up offer.

## Creative Strategy

- Write a concrete hook, not a slogan. Good hooks name a tension, mistake, result, visual surprise, or unanswered question.
- Use beat structure. Every beat should change what the viewer/listener knows, feels, or expects.
- Keep scripts speakable. Read them aloud and remove sentences that sound like written copy.
- Design visual variety intentionally: face, screen, product, diagram, B-roll, caption, zoom, object, before/after, or proof shot.
- Include accessibility by default: captions/subtitles, transcript or show notes, readable on-screen text, contrast, and alt/description notes when applicable.
- For educational content, sequence from problem recognition to insight to practical example to next action.

## Compliance And Risk

- Verify claims, demos, screenshots, customer examples, prices, and performance metrics.
- Flag music, footage, image, voice, guest, AI-generated media, and third-party rights.
- Do not imply Ink created, recorded, edited, uploaded, streamed, cleared rights, or collected metrics unless that tool was explicitly used.
- For sponsored content, paid creator work, or endorsements, include disclosure needs in the handoff.
- For live formats, include moderation and safety risks for chat, Q&A, sensitive topics, and attendee data.

## Manual Boundary

Ink can prepare scripts, outlines, episode briefs, run-of-show documents, shot lists, title/thumbnail options, descriptions, captions, show notes, and handoff notes. Recording, editing, hosting, uploading, music licensing, moderation, clipping, ad setup, and analytics remain manual unless a real tool is used.

## Metrics

- Short video: views, engaged views, hook retention, average watch time, completion rate, replays, shares, saves, follows, comments, and click-through.
- Long video: impressions, CTR, average view duration, retention curve, subscribers gained, comments, end-screen clicks, and search/suggested source.
- Audio: starts, consumption rate, followers, episode completion, downloads/listens, clips shared, and qualitative feedback.
- Webinar/livestream: registration rate, attendance rate, engagement score, watch time, Q&A, poll participation, CTA clicks, meetings booked, and on-demand views.

## QA Checklist

- The first beat earns attention without misleading.
- The promise in title/thumbnail/caption matches the actual content.
- Every section has a role in retention or comprehension.
- Rights, claims, captions, and accessibility needs are flagged.
- Manual production and publishing tasks are listed separately.
- Metrics match the surface and do not depend on invented data.

## Anti-Patterns

- Long brand intros before the value appears.
- Talking-head scripts that never use the visual medium.
- Overstuffed scripts with no breathing room.
- Misleading thumbnails or titles that create retention drop-off.
- Trend/audio use without brand fit or rights.
- Webinars with no interaction plan and podcasts with no premise beyond "talk about topic."

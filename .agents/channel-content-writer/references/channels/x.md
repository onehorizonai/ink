## Native Behavior

- X is a real-time public conversation graph. Posts are judged as statements, replies, jokes, evidence, updates, and participation in a live context.
- Brevity matters. Standard post copy is constrained, and links consume character space; every word needs a job.
- Replies, quote posts, reposts, and threads are distinct modes. A standalone post should not read like a reply with missing context.
- Visuals and video can carry proof, but the copy still needs to make the reason to click, watch, or reply clear.
- The audience is especially sensitive to corporate vagueness, engagement bait, and late commentary.

## Audience And Context Rules

- Identify whether the account is speaking as a founder, product, brand, community, support, or executive voice.
- Decide whether the post is news, opinion, launch, proof, tactical advice, thread, reply, live event update, customer-support note, or paid creative.
- For real-time topics, verify context before drafting. Do not attach the brand to a trend if the account has no credible role.
- For threads, each post should advance the idea and make sense if partially separated in the feed.
- For replies, answer the actual post and preserve conversational proportion. Do not hijack.

## Creative Strategy

- Put the point in the first line. Do not delay with "quick thought" or "we are excited to announce" unless the account voice demands it.
- Use concrete nouns, numbers, images, screenshots, or examples. X rewards specificity and quotable clarity.
- Write short threads only when the idea needs sequence. Default to a single post when possible.
- For launches, include what changed, who it helps, and a direct link or next action.
- For paid X placements, account for character limits, media specs, CTA button availability, and truncation risk.
- Draft likely replies when the post may generate objections, support questions, or debate.

## Compliance And Risk

- Avoid defamatory claims, unsupported competitor comparisons, financial/medical/legal advice, impersonation, and harassment.
- Disclose sponsorship, affiliate, paid partnership, employment, or material connection when relevant.
- Do not use fake urgency, fake outrage, engagement bait, or manufactured controversy.
- Flag screenshots containing personal data, private messages, customer names, unreleased features, or security details.

## Manual Boundary

Ink can draft posts, threads, reply options, quote-post framing, alt text, visual briefs, and moderation notes. A human must publish, schedule, attach media, verify live context, moderate replies, run paid setup, and collect analytics unless a real tool is used.

## Metrics

- Primary: qualified replies, reposts with favorable context, profile visits, link clicks, follows, community/customer responses, and downstream conversions.
- Secondary: impressions, likes, bookmarks, video views, engagement rate, and thread completion.
- Diagnostic: first-post engagement, reply sentiment, link versus no-link performance, media format, posting time around live context, and quote-post quality.

## QA Checklist

- The post has enough context to stand alone.
- It fits the account's role in the conversation.
- Character length, links, media, and truncation are checked for the selected placement.
- Claims, screenshots, disclosures, and sensitive context are verified or flagged.
- Reply/moderation notes are included for risky or high-interest posts.

## Anti-Patterns

- Corporate announcement copy with no point of view.
- Empty "thoughts?" closes, bait questions, and controversy farming.
- Threads that could have been one clear post.
- Trend hijacking without relevance.
- Screenshots that leak private or unsupported information.

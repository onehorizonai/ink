## Native Behavior

- Instagram is visual-first and mobile-first. Copy supports the asset; it rarely compensates for a weak first frame, cover, crop, or visual idea.
- Discovery differs by surface: Reels need immediate motion and retention, carousels need swipe value, feed posts need visual clarity, Stories need sequence and interaction, and profile grids need cover coherence.
- Instagram recommendations favor original, account-native creative over low-effort reposting or watermarked aggregation. Treat rights and originality as creative inputs, not afterthoughts.
- Captions are read after the viewer has already paused. The first visual and first caption line need to agree on the promise.
- Comments and DMs are often part of the conversion path. Draft suggested responses when the post invites questions, objections, or buying intent.
- DM automation should start from official or partner-supported triggers such as comment keywords, Story replies, inbound DMs, or eligible Follow-to-DM flows. Do not plan scraped follower outreach or browser-bot DMs.

## Audience And Context Rules

- Identify whether the audience is discovering the account cold, following for education, considering a product, already using the product, or reacting to a community moment.
- Choose the action the format is best at: save, share, comment, DM, click profile link, watch more, follow, or remember the brand.
- Do not force every Instagram post into a sales CTA. Many strong posts earn saves, shares, or DMs before direct conversion.
- Use account-native language. A founder account can be more personal; a brand account needs clearer editorial framing and visual consistency.
- For B2B Instagram, make abstract expertise visible through screens, workflows, before/after examples, people, diagrams, data, or artifacts.

## Creative Strategy

- Define the visual asset before finalizing the caption: subject, frame, movement, overlay text, safe area, cover, crop, alt text, and rights.
- Reels: open with visible action, result, mistake, contrast, or recognizable situation in the first second.
- Carousels: make slide 1 promise a useful payoff, make each slide independently legible, and end with a save/share/comment or next-step prompt.
- Feed images: use strong composition, readable text if any, real product/place/person signals, and contrast that survives mobile viewing.
- Captions: start with the same tension or result as the asset, then add context, proof, or a clean question. Avoid burying the point after throat-clearing.
- Hashtags: use a small relevant set only when they aid classification or discovery. Do not rely on hashtags to solve weak creative.
- Stories: script sequence, tap-forward rhythm, stickers/polls/questions, and DM prompts explicitly when relevant.

## DM Automation

- Load `../formats/social-dm-automation.md` whenever the requested Instagram output includes DMs, comment-to-DM, Story replies, follower welcome messages, inbox FAQ automation, download-link delivery, lead capture, or Manychat setup.
- Treat Follow-to-DM as a beta/eligibility-dependent follower welcome trigger. If the account is not eligible, use comment keywords, Story replies, or inbound DM keywords instead.
- First follower or keyword messages should usually ask or segment before sending a link. Send a direct link first only when the user explicitly asked for the link.
- Private replies to comments are opening messages; plan the flow so the user clicks a non-URL button or replies before any longer follow-up sequence.
- Keep automated welcome flows short: one opening question, one useful link or answer after interaction, and one optional question to continue the conversation.

## Compliance And Risk

- Flag music, footage, image, screenshot, testimonial, influencer, employee, and customer-content rights.
- Use platform paid-partnership tools and clear disclosure language where paid, gifted, affiliate, employee, or partner relationships could affect interpretation.
- Avoid fabricated results, before/after deception, fake comments, fake social proof, engagement bait, and misleading scarcity.
- Avoid cold follower blasts, browser automation, scraped lists, fake personalization, and repeated promotional DMs.
- Keep critical text away from UI overlays and make on-screen text readable without zooming.
- Flag sensitive topics, minors, health/finance claims, political content, competitor comparisons, and privacy/security screenshots.

## Manual Boundary

Ink can draft captions, Reels scripts, carousel copy, Story frames, cover concepts, alt text, comments/reply prompts, hashtag sets, DM automation copy, trigger maps, and production handoffs. A human or external partner tool must publish, schedule, select audio, clear rights, edit media, choose covers, tag accounts, apply paid-partnership labels, configure DM automation, verify account eligibility, moderate comments/DMs, and record analytics unless a real tool is used.

## Metrics

- Reels: retention, average watch time, completion, replays, shares, saves, follows, profile visits, and comments.
- Carousels: swipe-through, saves, shares, comments, profile visits, and slide drop-off when available.
- Feed/Stories: reach, replies, taps, link sticker/profile clicks, DMs, sticker interactions, follows, and downstream conversions.
- DM automation: trigger volume, messages sent, replies/button clicks, link clicks, installs/signups, human handoffs, opt-outs, negative replies, and tool-limit hits.
- Track creative variables: hook type, visual format, cover, topic, caption structure, CTA, posting context, and originality.

## QA Checklist

- The first visual frame or slide communicates the topic without relying on the caption.
- The caption, visual, and CTA have one coherent job.
- Rights, disclosures, tags, audio, and customer references are flagged.
- DM trigger, eligibility, messaging window, link destination, suppression rules, and handoff owner are listed when automation is part of the output.
- Text overlays fit mobile safe areas and are readable.
- Manual publish, cover, moderation, and analytics steps are listed.
- The output does not include internal labels in audience-facing copy.

## Anti-Patterns

- Reposting TikToks with watermarks and calling it an Instagram strategy.
- Decorative carousels where each slide repeats the same idea.
- Captions that read like LinkedIn posts pasted below a weak visual.
- Hashtag stuffing, engagement bait, fake trend participation, and generic "link in bio" CTAs.
- Over-polished brand videos that hide the actual product, person, or proof.

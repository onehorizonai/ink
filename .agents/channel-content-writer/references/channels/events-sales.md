# Events And Sales Channel Family Guidance

Use this family guide for conference talks, workshops, sales decks, one-pagers, case studies, and related enablement assets.

## Source Guidance

- ON24 webinar and digital engagement benchmark guidance: https://www.on24.com/webinar-benchmarks/
- Gartner sales enablement overview: https://www.gartner.com/smarterwithgartner/the-who-what-how-and-why-of-sales-enablement
- B2B case-study and customer-story references: https://www.scalevp.com/resources/building-a-great-b2b-case-study and https://www.arcade.software/post/how-to-write-a-case-study
- Product release and changelog communication references when event/sales assets discuss launches: https://www.intercom.com/blog/what-is-a-changelog/

## Native Behavior

- Events and sales assets are decision-support artifacts. They must help a person understand, trust, compare, remember, or act.
- Live talks and workshops need a narrative spine plus interaction plan. Sales decks and one-pagers need scanability and proof. Case studies need credibility and customer approval.
- These assets are often used by someone other than the writer. Include speaker/seller notes and approval boundaries.

## Audience And Context Rules

- Identify the audience's role, stage, and room context: conference attendee, workshop participant, executive buyer, technical evaluator, champion, procurement, partner, or internal sales team.
- Define the decision or behavior the asset should enable.
- Choose proof appropriate to the stage: category insight, workflow example, product walkthrough, customer story, metric, quote, demo, or risk reversal.
- For sales assets, map likely objections and buyer questions before writing.
- For case studies, confirm permission, naming, anonymization, metrics, and approval path.

## Creative Strategy

- Build around a spine: problem, stakes, insight, proof, action.
- Use contrast to clarify: before/after, old/new workflow, cost of inaction, alternative approaches, common mistake versus better model.
- Make every slide/page/section earn its place. Remove generic company filler unless it supports trust or decision-making.
- Use visuals for comprehension: diagrams, screenshots, customer quotes, process maps, timeline, before/after, and summary boxes.
- Include follow-up assets: email, one-pager, demo link, worksheet, recording, or CTA.

## Compliance And Risk

- Verify customer logos, quotes, metrics, screenshots, pricing, benchmark claims, competitor comparisons, and legal statements.
- Flag confidential sales-only content versus public-safe content.
- Avoid fabricated pipeline claims, anonymous "customer" proof that sounds fake, and unapproved use of customer names.
- For events, check speaker rules, sponsor disclosures, accessibility, recording permission, and content ownership.

## Manual Boundary

Ink can draft outlines, slide copy, talk tracks, proof plans, customer-story drafts, one-pager copy, design briefs, and review notes. Deck design, live delivery, workshop facilitation, customer approval, legal review, CRM updates, and publishing remain manual unless explicitly tooled.

## Metrics

- Events: registrations, attendance, engagement, Q&A, meetings booked, survey feedback, replay views, and follow-up conversions.
- Sales assets: usage by reps, buyer engagement, influenced opportunities, objection handling, deal-stage movement, and qualitative seller feedback.
- Case studies: page engagement, sales usage, demo conversion, influenced pipeline, quote reuse, and customer approval cycle time.

## QA Checklist

- Audience, stage, and desired decision are explicit.
- Proof is approved or clearly flagged.
- The artifact has a clear spine and no filler sections.
- Public versus internal usage is clear.
- Manual design, approval, delivery, and publishing tasks are separated.
- Follow-up or sales handoff is included.

## Anti-Patterns

- Feature tours pretending to be decks.
- Talks with no audience transformation.
- One-pagers that read like brochures.
- Case studies with vague "leading company" proof and no concrete before/after.
- Hiding caveats, approvals, or legal risk.
- Treating sales enablement as final page copy instead of decision support.

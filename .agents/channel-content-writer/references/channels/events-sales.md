Use this family guide for hosted events, attended events, webinars, livestreams, workshops, meetups, roundtables, hackathons, conference talks, conference booths, sponsored events, sales decks, one-pagers, case studies, and related enablement assets.

## Native Behavior

- Events are a marketing channel, not just a calendar item. They can create attention, trust, community, partner leverage, content, sales conversations, user research, recruiting signals, and customer education in one motion.
- Hosted events and attended events behave differently. Hosted events need audience acquisition, venue/logistics control, hospitality, sponsor management, run-of-show discipline, and follow-up. Attended events need selection rigor, meeting targets, field notes, content capture, sponsor/booth activation, and fast post-event routing.
- Sales assets are decision-support artifacts. They must help a person understand, trust, compare, remember, or act.
- Live talks and workshops need a narrative spine plus interaction plan. Meetups and roundtables need social design. Hackathons need operational resilience. Booths need pre-booked meetings and a qualification path. Sales decks and one-pagers need scanability and proof. Case studies need credibility and customer approval.
- These assets are often used by someone other than the writer. Include speaker, seller, host, moderator, sponsor, and operator notes with approval boundaries.

## Audience And Context Rules

- Identify the audience's role, stage, and room context: registrant, no-show risk, conference attendee, booth visitor, workshop participant, meetup regular, first-time community member, founder, developer, executive buyer, technical evaluator, champion, procurement, partner, sponsor, speaker, or internal sales team.
- Define the decision or behavior the asset should enable.
- Choose proof appropriate to the stage: category insight, workflow example, product walkthrough, customer story, metric, quote, demo, or risk reversal.
- For hosted events, define the attendance promise before the topic. The promise should explain why this room, this date, and this speaker mix are worth a commute or live calendar block.
- For attended events, define the reason to be there: meetings booked, partner development, lead capture, talent/recruiting, content capture, community trust, investor visibility, customer education, or speaker authority.
- For sales assets, map likely objections and buyer questions before writing.
- For case studies, confirm permission, naming, anonymization, metrics, and approval path.

## Creative Strategy

- Build around a spine: problem, stakes, insight, proof, action.
- Use contrast to clarify: before/after, old/new workflow, cost of inaction, alternative approaches, common mistake versus better model.
- Make every slide/page/section earn its place. Remove generic company filler unless it supports trust or decision-making.
- Use visuals for comprehension: diagrams, screenshots, customer quotes, process maps, timeline, before/after, and summary boxes.
- Include follow-up assets: email, one-pager, demo link, worksheet, recording, or CTA.
- Treat the event as a content system. Plan pre-event demand, speaker/partner kits, live capture, attendee notes, post-event recap, replay, sales handoff, community invite, and next-event teaser before the event happens.
- Design the room for the behavior you want. Classroom seating supports teaching, cafe tables support exercises, circles support roundtables, standing space supports networking, and booths need traffic flow plus a private conversation escape.
- Plan hospitality as conversion design. Arrival signage, check-in speed, coffee/water, dietary labeling, alcohol policy, quiet corners, badge legibility, and a warm host change how much trust people give the content.
- Use partner brands deliberately. A sponsor logo can reduce registration friction, but the attendee promise still has to stand on its own.

## Compliance And Risk

- Verify customer logos, partner logos, sponsor terms, quotes, metrics, screenshots, pricing, benchmark claims, competitor comparisons, and legal statements.
- Flag confidential sales-only content versus public-safe content.
- Avoid fabricated pipeline claims, anonymous "customer" proof that sounds fake, and unapproved use of customer names.
- For events, check speaker rules, sponsor disclosures, accessibility, recording permission, image/video consent, badge scanning consent, food allergies, alcohol rules, venue insurance, AV constraints, cancellation terms, and content ownership.
- For free events with sponsor reimbursement, separate committed costs, eligible actual costs, receipts, reimbursement cap, required sponsor recognition, and any rules about attendee charging.
- For hackathons, add safety, overnight supervision if relevant, minors/age policy, code of conduct, prize terms, judging fairness, IP ownership, hardware safety, and mentor conduct.
- For attended conferences, respect event organizer rules for badge scanning, unofficial side events, filming, swag, contests, room drops, and sponsor claims.

## Manual Boundary

Ink can draft event strategy briefs, talk outlines, workshop plans, registration copy, speaker kits, sponsor notes, venue RFP notes, run-of-show docs, moderator guides, booth scripts, follow-up sequences, sales handoff notes, slide copy, proof plans, customer-story drafts, one-pager copy, design briefs, and review notes.

Venue booking, catering orders, beverage purchase, contracts, insurance, payment, travel, AV setup, badge scanning, live delivery, workshop facilitation, booth staffing, customer approval, legal review, CRM updates, publishing, and reimbursement submission remain manual unless explicitly tooled.

## Metrics

- Hosted events: registration source, registration conversion, attendance rate, attendee quality, waitlist, check-in rate, arrival time distribution, session engagement, Q&A, meetings booked, survey score, opt-ins, community joins, replay views, content reuse, and follow-up conversions.
- Attended events: target account coverage, pre-booked meetings, meetings completed, booth scans, qualified conversations, partner intros, speaker/session attendance, content captured, follow-up SLA, pipeline sourced or influenced, and qualitative field notes.
- Hospitality and operations: venue cost, food and beverage cost per attendee, no-show waste, setup time, AV issues, dietary misses, sponsor deliverables completed, incident count, and reimbursement status.
- Sales assets: usage by reps, buyer engagement, influenced opportunities, objection handling, deal-stage movement, and qualitative seller feedback.
- Case studies: page engagement, sales usage, demo conversion, influenced pipeline, quote reuse, and customer approval cycle time.

## QA Checklist

- Audience, stage, and desired decision are explicit.
- Host versus attend posture is explicit.
- Event promise, audience acquisition path, registration mechanics, and follow-up owner are defined.
- Venue, AV, food, beverage, accessibility, staffing, sponsor, budget, and risk assumptions are listed when the event is hosted.
- Meeting goals, account list, booth/speaker plan, content capture, and lead-routing rules are listed when the event is attended.
- Proof is approved or clearly flagged.
- The artifact has a clear spine and no filler sections.
- Public versus internal usage is clear.
- Manual design, approval, delivery, and publishing tasks are separated.
- Follow-up or sales handoff is included.

## Anti-Patterns

- Feature tours pretending to be decks.
- Talks with no audience transformation.
- Events planned around what the company wants to say instead of why the audience would show up.
- Treating free attendance as free attention. Free events still need a sharp promise, reminders, and a room experience worth the time.
- Forgetting no-shows, dietary needs, AV testing, signage, host scripts, receipts, or follow-up ownership.
- Attending conferences with no pre-booked meetings, target list, booth message, or next-day routing.
- One-pagers that read like brochures.
- Case studies with vague "leading company" proof and no concrete before/after.
- Hiding caveats, approvals, or legal risk.
- Treating sales enablement as final page copy instead of decision support.

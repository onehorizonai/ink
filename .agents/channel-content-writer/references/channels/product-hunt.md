# Product Hunt Channel Guidance

Use this guide for `product-hunt` after loading `communities.md`. Pair with `../formats/community-post.md` and launch-specific assets such as maker comments, tagline options, gallery captions, and reply prompts.

## Source Guidance

- Product Hunt Launch Guide: https://www.producthunt.com/launch/
- Product Hunt launch dashboard and launch-day education: https://www.producthunt.com/launch

## Native Behavior

- Product Hunt is a launch community, not a generic press-release surface. Makers are expected to explain what they built, why it matters, and respond personally.
- The first impression is compact: product name, tagline, thumbnail/gallery, category, and maker comment.
- Launch-day momentum depends on preparation, community relevance, clear positioning, and thoughtful replies, not only the launch post.
- Product Hunt users often evaluate novelty, build quality, founder clarity, and usefulness quickly.
- Feedback is public. Plan responses to bugs, pricing questions, comparisons, roadmap suggestions, and skeptical comments.

## Audience And Context Rules

- Identify target voters/commenters: builders, founders, operators, designers, developers, investors, early adopters, or category specialists.
- Clarify what is genuinely new, useful, or timely. Avoid "AI for X" positioning unless the use case is concrete.
- Explain who the product is not for. Launch communities respect focus more than generic breadth.
- Use the maker perspective. Personal build context usually performs better than faceless marketing copy.
- If the launch needs support from existing users or partners, the handoff must avoid vote-buying, spammy asks, or misleading incentives.

## Creative Strategy

- Tagline: one crisp use case or promise, not a slogan.
- Maker comment: concise story of problem, insight, what exists now, who it helps, and what feedback is wanted.
- Gallery: lead with product proof. Screenshots, demo GIFs, short videos, and feature captions should answer "what is it?" immediately.
- Comment replies: be generous, specific, and transparent. Thank, answer, ask clarifying questions, and note roadmap items without overpromising.
- Launch-day updates: share meaningful milestones, answers, or resources, not repetitive vote asks.

## Compliance And Risk

- Do not create fake accounts, buy votes, coordinate deceptive upvotes, or imply endorsements that do not exist.
- Flag claims about customer numbers, revenue, rankings, security, integrations, pricing, and availability.
- Disclose maker, investor, employee, affiliate, or partner relationships when relevant.
- Avoid attacking competitors or positioning the launch through unsupported comparisons.

## Manual Boundary

Ink can draft tagline options, maker comments, gallery captions, FAQ replies, launch-day update copy, outreach notes, and risk checks. A human must submit the product, upload assets, coordinate makers, respond live, manage community outreach, and collect launch metrics.

## Metrics

- Product Hunt rank and upvotes are visible signals, but do not treat them as the only success metric.
- Track comments, quality of feedback, qualified signups, trial starts, demo requests, waitlist growth, investor/partner interest, traffic quality, and post-launch retention.
- Diagnose by source: Product Hunt profile, social amplification, email/community outreach, press, founder network, and direct traffic.

## QA Checklist

- Product name, tagline, thumbnail, gallery, and maker comment all explain the same promise.
- The maker comment sounds like a real builder and asks for useful feedback.
- Claims, availability, pricing, integrations, and roadmap statements are verified or flagged.
- Reply prompts cover praise, critique, bugs, pricing, comparison, and roadmap comments.
- Manual launch ownership and live-response expectations are explicit.

## Anti-Patterns

- Press-release copy in the maker comment.
- Vote-begging, giveaways for upvotes, or spammy outreach.
- Taglines that describe a category but not the product's use.
- Gallery images that look pretty but do not show the product.
- Overpromising roadmap items in public comments.

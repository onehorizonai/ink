## Native Behavior

- YouTube is both a search engine and a recommendation surface. A video must satisfy the promise created by its title, thumbnail, first seconds, and viewer intent.
- Long-form discovery often starts with title/thumbnail; retention starts with the opening. Do not write outlines before clarifying the click promise.
- Shorts are swipe-first, mobile-first, and increasingly can be up to three minutes depending on upload date and classification. They need immediate context and a clean loop or payoff.
- YouTube viewers tolerate depth when pacing, structure, and payoff are clear. They punish mismatch between promise and content.
- Descriptions, chapters, captions, comments, playlists, pinned comments, and end screens are distribution and comprehension assets, not filler.

## Audience And Context Rules

- Identify the viewer intent: searching for a solution, evaluating alternatives, learning a concept, watching a story, following a personality, or browsing Shorts.
- Choose the video role: tutorial, demo, review, comparison, teardown, opinion, documentary, update, clip, live replay, or community answer.
- For long-form, define what the viewer should know or be able to do by the end.
- For Shorts, define the single retention job: surprise, quick win, mistake, reveal, proof, or repeatable insight.
- For B2B, use concrete screens, workflows, examples, and decisions. Avoid abstract brand language.

## Creative Strategy

- Draft title and thumbnail concepts before the full outline. They should express one promise without misleading.
- Start the video by confirming the viewer clicked the right thing, then move quickly into value. Avoid long branded intros.
- Use retention beats: open loop, proof shot, pattern break, example, objection, recap, and next step.
- For long-form, include chapter titles, B-roll/screen needs, demo assets, citations, and pinned-comment or description links.
- For Shorts, write a first-second visual and line, a beat-by-beat script, readable on-screen text, and a cut pattern.
- Make descriptions useful: one-line summary, relevant links, chapters when appropriate, credits, disclosures, and next video/resource.

## Compliance And Risk

- Verify claims, product demos, customer examples, benchmark numbers, prices, and competitor comparisons.
- Flag music, footage, screenshots, third-party clips, guest releases, AI voices, synthetic media, and copyrighted assets.
- Do not use misleading thumbnails, false scarcity, fake reactions, or bait titles that the video does not pay off.
- Add sponsorship, affiliate, gifted-product, or partner disclosure notes when needed.
- For comments/community, flag moderation needs for sensitive topics.

## Manual Boundary

Ink can prepare outlines, scripts, title/thumbnail concepts, descriptions, chapter plans, pinned comments, Shorts captions, and production QA. A human must record, edit, upload, design thumbnails, clear rights, configure monetization/disclosures, moderate comments, and collect analytics unless a real tool is used.

## Metrics

- Long-form: impressions, CTR, average view duration, retention curve, returning viewers, subscribers gained, comments, likes, end-screen clicks, search/suggested source, and conversions.
- Shorts: viewed versus swiped away, engaged views, watch time, completion, rewatches, likes, comments, shares, subscribers gained, and profile/channel actions.
- Diagnostic: title/thumbnail test learning, opening drop-off, chapter drop-off, traffic source, topic fit, and comment quality.

## QA Checklist

- Title, thumbnail concept, opening, and content promise match.
- The first 15 to 30 seconds of long-form remove uncertainty and start value.
- Shorts are legible in the first second and work vertically on mobile.
- Descriptions, links, disclosures, captions, chapters, and pinned comments are planned when relevant.
- Rights, claims, and manual production tasks are flagged.

## Anti-Patterns

- Clickbait that creates a retention cliff.
- Long intros, logos, or backstory before the viewer gets value.
- Blog-post outlines with no visual plan.
- Shorts that require the caption to understand the video.
- Descriptions stuffed with keywords but no viewer utility.

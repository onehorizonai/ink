# Communities Channel Family Guidance

Use this family guide for community-native posts, Q&A, discussion prompts, launch comments, and forum answers.

## Native Behavior

- Communities are trust systems first and content surfaces second. A good post fits the local norms before it optimizes conversion.
- Moderation risk is part of the medium. Rules, flair, title style, self-promotion tolerance, link policy, and account history can determine whether a draft survives.
- Communities reward specificity, humility, usefulness, and real participation. They punish obvious funnels, generic advice, and polished brand intrusion.
- Discussion quality matters more than surface engagement. The best outcome may be objections, language, referrals, or learning rather than clicks.

## Audience And Context Rules

- Identify the community's shared identity: builders, operators, customers, hobbyists, experts, job seekers, founders, developers, buyers, or fans.
- Ask why the post belongs there now. A launch, field note, question, teardown, useful resource, support answer, or transparent ask each needs different framing.
- Determine whether the poster is a member, brand, founder, employee, partner, or outsider. The same words land differently depending on identity.
- Read recent top posts and rules when possible. If research has not been done, mark community fit as a risk.
- For Q&A, answer the actual question before adding context or optional product relevance.

## Creative Strategy

- Lead with the community's problem or curiosity, not the sender's desired CTA.
- Use concrete detail: numbers, tradeoffs, screenshots, process, failure, constraints, or exact question.
- Make product mentions proportionate and late unless the community expects launch/feedback posts.
- Use plain titles that sound like the venue. Do not import headline styles from ads or LinkedIn.
- Invite discussion with a real prompt: compare approaches, ask for experience, request critique, or offer a useful artifact.
- Include a "what not to include" section in handoffs for risky communities.

## Compliance And Risk

- Respect platform and community rules on self-promotion, spam, harassment, misinformation, adult/sensitive content, automation, vote manipulation, and undisclosed affiliations.
- Do not imply independent endorsement if the poster has a material connection.
- Avoid astroturfing, fake user stories, fake questions, fake "just found this" posts, or planted comments.
- Flag regulated claims, competitor claims, customer data, private screenshots, and personal information.

## Manual Boundary

Ink can draft posts, answers, titles, comments, moderation-risk notes, launch notes, and handoffs. Posting, account participation, comment moderation, vote management, follow-up relationships, rule appeals, and community research beyond available tools remain manual.

## Metrics

- Primary: quality replies, objections discovered, accepted answers, referrals, qualified conversations, product feedback, moderation outcome, and community trust.
- Secondary: upvotes/reactions, comments, traffic, signups, followers, launch ranking, and shares.
- Track language and objections as reusable insight for future content, positioning, and sales materials.

## QA Checklist

- Community rules and recent norms are checked or marked unknown.
- The title/opener sounds native.
- The post gives value before asking for value.
- Product/brand mentions are proportionate and disclosed when needed.
- Link, screenshot, and CTA choices comply with local norms.
- Follow-up/comment handling is included for likely replies.

## Anti-Patterns

- "We built X, thoughts?" with no community-specific reason.
- Asking for feedback while only wanting traffic.
- Posting polished ad copy in a discussion venue.
- Ending every post with a link, demo request, or "DM me."
- Using fake personal stories, hidden affiliation, or vote/comment coordination.
- Ignoring negative replies instead of responding usefully or learning from them.

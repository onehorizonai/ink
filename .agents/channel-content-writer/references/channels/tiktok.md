# TikTok Channel Guidance

Use this guide for `tiktok` and `tiktok-video` after loading `social.md` and `video-audio.md`. Pair it with `../formats/short-video.md`.

## Native Behavior

- TikTok is discovery-led. Most viewers are not waiting for the brand, so the first second must make the situation, emotion, or payoff obvious.
- Native TikTok language is visual, conversational, and culturally current. It rewards specific human texture more than polished brand exposition.
- Search behavior matters. Users increasingly discover how-tos, reviews, explanations, and product answers through TikTok search, so captions and spoken/on-screen keywords should match real user phrasing.
- Comments, stitches, duets, sounds, creator conventions, and trends can be content inputs, but each needs rights, brand fit, and timing.
- Good TikToks often feel like a person showing or noticing something, not a company making an announcement.

## Audience And Context Rules

- Identify the viewer's starting frame: bored scrolling, researching a problem, comparing products, seeking entertainment, following a creator, or reacting to a trend.
- Decide whether the post should entertain, teach, demonstrate, review, debunk, show behind the scenes, recruit, or drive action.
- Use audience language from the niche. Avoid executive phrasing when the format wants direct speech.
- For B2B TikTok, translate abstract business pain into visible everyday moments: tabs, tickets, dashboards, meetings, handoffs, mistakes, and relief.
- Do not chase every trend. Use trends only when the account can add a new angle or credible participation.

## Creative Strategy

- Open on movement, a face, product action, a surprising object, a mistake, a result, or a line that creates immediate context.
- Use simple beat structure: setup, tension, reveal, proof/example, payoff, next action.
- Write scripts to be spoken. Remove sentences that sound like an ad read unless the concept intentionally uses ad-read parody.
- Use on-screen text as navigation, not subtitles for every thought. Captions/subtitles still matter for accessibility and sound-off viewing.
- Give the editor shot notes: camera position, cuts, zooms, B-roll, screen recordings, captions, sound cue, and where the hook should appear.
- For creator-style content, brief tone, premise, proof points, mandatory claims/disclosures, and creative freedom separately.

## Compliance And Risk

- Flag music/sound rights, creator agreements, paid partnership disclosure, product claims, before/after claims, giveaways, and regulated categories.
- Avoid deceptive UGC, fake reviews, undisclosed employees, fake urgency, and "everyone is switching" social proof without evidence.
- Do not instruct engagement manipulation, spam comments, mass DMs, or misleading duet/stitch behavior.
- If using trends, verify the trend's meaning and potential brand-safety implications before publishing.

## Manual Boundary

Ink can draft scripts, captions, hook variants, shot lists, creator briefs, comment-response prompts, and manual QA notes. A human must record, edit, select sounds, clear rights, post, tag/disclose, moderate, and evaluate analytics unless an explicit tool performs those steps.

## Metrics

- Hook hold, watch time, completion, rewatches, shares, saves, comments, profile visits, follows, search impressions, clicks, and conversions.
- Track creative variables: hook type, creator/persona, topic, sound/trend, length, on-screen text, visual format, and CTA.
- For paid TikTok, track thumb-stop, CTR, CVR, CPA/ROAS, creative fatigue, and comment sentiment.

## QA Checklist

- The first second makes the idea legible.
- The script sounds like a person and shows something concrete.
- The caption and spoken/on-screen keywords match the topic users would search for.
- Rights, disclosure, claims, and trend context are flagged.
- Manual production and publish tasks are separate from the draft.
- The CTA is natural for TikTok: comment, save, follow, search, click, DM, or try.

## Anti-Patterns

- Repurposing a LinkedIn post as a talking-head script with no visual change.
- Overproduced corporate intros, logo stings, or brand manifestos before value appears.
- Trend use that is late, unrelated, or culturally misunderstood.
- Dense captions that belong in a newsletter.
- Fake UGC, fake testimonials, and undisclosed incentives.

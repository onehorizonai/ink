# Social Channel Family Guidance

Use this family guide for social feeds and social-native visual surfaces before loading a specific channel or format guide.

## Native Behavior

- Social posts are consumed in motion: readers scan the first line, first frame, visual shape, account identity, and social proof before deciding whether to pause.
- Each platform has a different "native grammar": Instagram rewards visual clarity and original assets, X rewards concise real-time takes and replies, Pinterest behaves closer to visual search, and decentralized networks such as Bluesky/Mastodon are especially sensitive to community norms and moderation context.
- Links, hashtags, mentions, reposts, and first comments are not universal defaults. Use them only when they improve discovery, attribution, or next action on the selected platform.
- Social outputs often live next to comments. Draft with reply paths in mind, not just the initial post.

## Audience And Context Rules

- Identify the audience's current state: unaware, problem-aware, solution-aware, customer, partner, community member, applicant, investor, or peer.
- Decide the job of the post before writing: spark recognition, teach one thing, show proof, start a discussion, announce, recruit, distribute a larger asset, or capture demand.
- Match the account's relationship with the audience. A founder account can use personal judgment; a brand account needs clearer utility and restraint.
- Use one core idea per post unless the format is a carousel, thread, list, or recap.
- Treat comments as part of the channel. Include suggested replies when the post is likely to attract objections, technical questions, or buying-intent questions.

## Creative Strategy

- Open with the strongest specific signal: tension, useful result, sharp observation, timely trigger, visual proof, or audience self-recognition.
- Make the asset do work. Visuals should clarify the idea, prove the result, or create pattern interruption. Do not attach decorative stock-like visuals.
- Keep copy proportional to format: short posts should land quickly; longer posts need a clear payoff and scannable rhythm.
- Use platform-native interaction levers only when earned: polls for real decisions, questions for real discussion, saves/shares for reusable assets, and comments for useful follow-up.
- For visual platforms, define the image/video brief alongside copy: subject, frame, contrast, text overlay, safe area, alt text, and rights.
- Preserve brand voice without sanding away platform fit. A post can sound like the selected profile and still obey the channel's reading behavior.

## Compliance And Risk

- Clearly disclose sponsored, affiliate, gifted, paid, employment, or partner relationships when they may affect how a reader interprets the post.
- Do not fabricate endorsements, "customer said" quotes, rankings, results, scarcity, or urgency.
- Avoid platform manipulation: fake engagement, vote/comment bait, deceptive account behavior, mass unsolicited posting, or undisclosed automation.
- Flag regulated topics such as finance, health, employment, housing, politics, children, data/privacy, and claims about competitors.
- Include alt text or accessibility notes when the output uses images, carousels, or video.

## Manual Boundary

Ink can draft posts, captions, threads, variants, reply prompts, alt text, visual briefs, and publishing handoffs. Publishing, scheduling, moderation, rights checks, boosting, paid setup, and analytics collection remain manual unless a real tool is explicitly used.

## Metrics

- Primary: qualified replies, saves, shares, clicks, comments with substance, profile visits, follows, and downstream conversions.
- Secondary: reach, impressions, reactions, reposts, hashtag discovery, and engagement rate.
- For visual/video posts, track asset-specific learnings: first frame, hook, topic, format, thumbnail/cover, caption structure, and posting context.
- Do not declare success from vanity reach alone. A smaller post that generates qualified replies may beat a large generic one.

## QA Checklist

- The destination slug is resolved in `channel-format-registry.csv`.
- The post has one clear job and one primary audience.
- The first visible line or visual frame gives a reason to stop.
- The asset/caption/link/hashtag choices are native to the selected platform.
- Any claim, endorsement, statistic, customer name, or competitor comparison is verified or flagged.
- Manual publishing, scheduling, paid setup, and moderation work are clearly separated from the final copy.

## Anti-Patterns

- Pasting LinkedIn-style line breaks into every social surface.
- Decorative images that do not clarify or prove the point.
- Empty "thoughts?", "agree?", or "anyone else?" closes.
- Hashtag stuffing, irrelevant trends, competitor tagging without purpose, or engagement bait.
- Using screenshots or customer content without rights or context.
- Treating all social channels as distribution pipes instead of distinct communities.

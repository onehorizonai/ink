# Instagram Reels Guidance

Use this reference for Instagram Reels, Instagram-first vertical video, and Content Program runs whose channel is `instagram-reels`.

## Sources To Consider

- Meta Best Practices: optimize creation, engagement, reach, monetization, and guidelines from the professional dashboard: https://about.fb.com/news/2024/10/best-practices-education-hub-creators-instagram/
- Meta original-content guidance: recommendation systems favor eligible original content and can limit reposted or low-quality content: https://about.fb.com/br/news/2024/04/ajudando-o-criador-de-conteudo-a-encontrar-novos-publicos/
- Instagram Reel size guidance: Reels support vertical formats and should use at least 30 FPS and 720p: https://www.facebook.com/help/instagram/1038071743007909
- Meta Reels insights: use available tests and track retention, replays, non-follower reach, and distribution quality: https://about.fb.com/news/2023/11/helping-creators-test-content-and-earn-rewards/
- Current social SEO guidance: captions, on-screen text, subtitles, and hashtags should reinforce the same topic naturally.

## Creation Rules

- Create for Instagram first. Do not reuse a TikTok export, visible watermark, border-only repost, unedited clip, or low-effort aggregation.
- Use 9:16 vertical video when possible, ideally 1080x1920, MP4 or MOV, at least 30 FPS, with highest-quality upload enabled.
- Put critical hook text, faces, product detail, and CTA cues in the visual safe area, away from top navigation and bottom caption/action overlays. A conservative Reels safe-zone heuristic is to keep critical elements out of the top 14%, bottom 35%, and outer 6% side edges.
- Make the first frame understandable before playback starts. Add motion or visual change immediately, not after a long setup.
- Keep the video as short as the idea allows. Prefer a tight 7 to 30 second execution for simple hooks and stay under 90 seconds unless the narrative truly needs more time.
- Keep hook overlay text short enough to read in one glance. Prefer one precise promise, tension, or recognition over generic hype.
- Include subtitles or on-screen text for any spoken value. The Reel should still make sense with sound off.
- Use trending or recognizable audio only when it fits the idea and the creator or brand has the right to use it.
- Choose a cover frame or thumbnail that can work in grid/profile previews and still communicates the topic after cropping.

## Caption And Search Rules

- Treat the caption as a searchable description, not a dumping ground. Make the first line compelling before the hidden "more" break.
- Use natural keywords in the caption, overlay text, subtitles, and hashtags so Instagram can classify the Reel accurately.
- Cover one primary topic and a small cluster of adjacent terms. Avoid keyword stuffing, unrelated trend terms, and repeated synonyms that make the copy read like SEO sludge.
- Use a specific save, share, or comment prompt only when it follows from the content value. Do not use empty engagement bait.
- Place hashtags in the caption unless the program says otherwise.
- Use exactly the number of hashtags required by the output brief. When the brief does not specify a count, keep hashtags restrained and highly relevant.

## Hashtag Selection

When a run needs 5 hashtags, choose a balanced set:

1. One niche audience or community tag.
2. One topic or category tag.
3. One problem, use-case, or intent tag.
4. One format or content-type tag.
5. One branded, campaign, product, or highly specific long-tail tag when available.

Every hashtag must be relevant to the actual Reel. Do not add broad vanity tags, banned or inactive tags, competitor tags without a reason, or tags that promise a different topic than the video delivers.

## Manual QA Before Publishing

- Preview on a phone before publishing and verify text is readable, not hidden by the Reels UI, and not cropped in the grid.
- Confirm footage, music, images, AI assets, and third-party clips are original, licensed, or otherwise approved for use.
- Check Account Status and recommendation eligibility when reach unexpectedly drops.
- Use Trial Reels, caption or thumbnail tests, or manual A/B variants when the account has access and the content risk justifies it.
- After publishing, respond to early comments with useful replies. If a Reel takes off, plan the next related Reel quickly.

## Metrics To Track

- Reach and non-follower reach.
- Plays, replays, average watch time, completion rate, and retention drop-off.
- Sends, shares, saves, comments, likes, profile visits, follows, and clicks when relevant.
- Distribution score or account-native comparison metrics when available.
- Notes on hook, cover, audio, caption structure, hashtag set, and publish timing so future runs can learn from the result.

# Style Capture

## Use a Small, Relevant Set

Build the voice profile from 3-5 examples that match:

- a similar topic
- a similar audience
- a similar thesis shape
- a similar CTA intensity

Use those examples to study voice and structure, not to inherit sources or citations.

Do not overfit to one unusually successful article unless the user explicitly wants that style.

## Capture These Markers

- title pattern
- opening setup
- sentence rhythm
- recurring sentence shapes or phrases that should not be repeated blindly
- paragraph length and spacing
- heading style
- level of specificity
- amount of tech-magazine or reportorial edge
- amount of narrative versus analysis
- proof density
- whether the article stays prose-led or uses lists sparingly
- CTA style
- how the article uses images, callouts, buttons, footnotes, or code

## Write a Short Voice Profile

Before drafting, summarize the voice in 6 lines or fewer:

- Usually opens by:
- Usually sounds:
- Usually proves points with:
- Usually avoids:
- Usually closes by:
- Repetition risks:

## Watch for Format Drift

Do not assume a personal founder story sounds the same as a product argument or a market analysis piece.

- some articles open with a sharp thesis
- some articles open with a concrete scenario
- some articles lean on product examples and screenshots
- some articles stay more reflective and less product-led

## Avoid These Failure Modes

- copying sentence fragments from a single exemplar
- repeating the same opening move, section rhythm, metaphor, or close across articles
- defaulting to generic SEO-blog cliches
- adding intensity that the archive does not support
- forcing visuals, footnotes, or components that the brief does not need
- inflating claims because the prose sounds more persuasive
- treating old article source notes as reusable research
- defaulting to safe SaaS-blog tone when the draft should feel sharper and more editorial
- letting bullet points flatten a piece that should read as flowing prose

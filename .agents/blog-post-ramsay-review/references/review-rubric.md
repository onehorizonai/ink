# Review Rubric

Use this rubric to keep the review brutal but useful.

## Core question

Ask:

- would the target reader keep going
- would they learn something specific
- would they trust the author
- would they feel this was worth their time

If the answer is mostly no, say it plainly.

## Score

Score the draft out of 15.

- 0-3: bin it
- 4-6: bland and undercooked
- 7-9: serviceable but forgettable
- 10-12: solid with clear fixes
- 13-15: strong and worth publishing

## Publish gate

Translate the score into a plain verdict.

- 0-6: do not publish
- 7-9: do not publish until substantial fixes land
- 10-12: publish only after targeted edits
- 13-15: publishable now

Any `Must Fix` issue overrides the score. If blocking issues remain unresolved, the article is not ready to publish even if the numeric score is otherwise decent.

## Categories

Score each category from 0-3.

### 1. Hook and momentum

- 0: slow, generic, or instantly skippable
- 1: understandable but flat
- 2: clear and reasonably engaging
- 3: sharp opening with real pull

### 2. Argument and structure

- 0: point is muddy and sections wander
- 1: basic spine exists but repeats or drifts
- 2: argument mostly advances section by section
- 3: tight structure with no obvious dead weight

### 3. Specificity and proof

- 0: empty abstractions and unsupported claims
- 1: some specifics but still padded or vague
- 2: enough proof to stay credible
- 3: concrete, original, and well-supported

### 4. Voice and texture

- 0: obvious AI slop or generic SEO sludge
- 1: readable but sterile or over-polished
- 2: sounds like a real person with a point
- 3: distinct voice without forced gimmicks

### 5. Reader payoff

- 0: reader gets little or nothing
- 1: some value, not enough to justify the length
- 2: useful and mostly worth the read
- 3: clear payoff that earns attention

## Must-fix triggers

Treat these as high-priority failures:

- the opening says nothing specific
- multiple sections repeat the same point
- the article sounds generated instead of lived or argued
- the draft uses filler to fake depth
- the close lands weakly or says what the piece already said
- the reader payoff is smaller than the time cost

Every must-fix issue needs evidence and a concrete fix. Do not just name the problem.
Every must-fix issue is blocking until it is addressed in the draft or explicitly waived by the user.

Use this issue format:

- `Problem:` what is wrong
- `Why it fails:` why the reader will care
- `Evidence:` short quote, section label, or precise location
- `Fix:` exact editorial action

When revisiting a revised draft, check each prior must-fix item explicitly. Do not assume it was handled unless the text now proves it.

## Style of feedback

- use short verdicts
- be direct
- swear only when it sharpens the diagnosis
- give fixes that can actually be applied
- keep praise brief and specific

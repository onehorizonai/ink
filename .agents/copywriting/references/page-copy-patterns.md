## Above the Fold

Use the first screen to answer three questions fast:

- what is this
- why should I care
- what should I do next

Build the hero from:

- headline
- subheadline
- primary CTA
- one proof cue or reassurance cue when it reduces hesitation

Keep the hero specific. Do not make the first screen do every job on the page.

## Core Persuasive Sections

Mix the sections that best support the argument. Do not force every section onto every page.

### Social Proof

Use one or two proof devices early when credibility matters:

- customer logos
- a key metric
- a short testimonial
- a rating or review summary

### Problem or Pain

Show the audience you understand the situation:

- describe the concrete frustration
- make the cost of the problem visible
- avoid abstract "inefficiency" language

### Solution or Benefits

Bridge from the problem to the offer:

- focus on the strongest 3 to 5 benefits
- connect each capability to an outcome
- attach proof where possible

### How It Works

Reduce perceived complexity with a short sequence:

- keep it to 3 or 4 steps
- pair each step with the outcome it unlocks

### Objection Handling

Answer the questions that block action:

- is this right for me
- how is this different
- is setup difficult
- what happens if it does not work

Use the format that fits the page:

- FAQ
- comparison block
- guarantee or risk-reversal block
- setup-clarity or implementation block

### Final CTA

Close by:

- restating the value clearly
- repeating the primary CTA
- adding real reassurance when it helps

## Landing-Page Section Variety

A strong landing page is rarely just a hero plus a stack of features. Mix section types to create movement.

Useful section types:

- social-proof bar
- problem section
- how-it-works steps
- benefits block
- testimonial
- use cases
- personas or "built for" section
- comparison to alternatives
- case-study snippet
- FAQ
- integrations or partner logos
- pricing preview
- guarantee or risk-reversal section

Avoid pages that read like this:

1. hero
2. feature
3. feature
4. feature
5. CTA

Prefer pages that vary the proof and argument:

1. hero
2. proof
3. problem
4. how it works
5. benefits
6. testimonial
7. use cases or personas
8. comparison or objection handling
9. FAQ
10. final CTA

## Page-Type Guidance

### Homepage

- lead with the broadest value proposition
- help multiple visitor intents without becoming generic
- create clear paths for different audiences or readiness levels

### Landing Page

- keep one message and one dominant CTA
- match the promise to the traffic source
- cut distractions that weaken the main action

### Pricing Page

- help the reader choose, not just compare columns
- clarify what is included
- reduce "which plan is right for me" anxiety
- make the recommended choice obvious when honest to do so

### Feature Page

- connect the feature to a practical outcome
- show examples or use cases
- explain how it differs from the status quo or alternatives
- keep a clear path to try or buy

### About Page

- explain why the company exists
- connect the story to the customer benefit
- build trust through specificity and transparency
- keep a real CTA because it is still a working page

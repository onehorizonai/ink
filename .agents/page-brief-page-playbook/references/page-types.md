# Page Types

Use this file when a page brief needs page-type defaults beyond what the live site or competitor pages already show.

## Selection Order

Choose the page family by job:

1. what the page must help the visitor do
2. what action the page should drive next
3. what surrounding pages already exist on the site

If two page families both fit, choose the dominant job and record the conflict in the brief.

## Universal Rules

- Give each page one clear primary job.
- Match the CTA to the page's job and funnel stage.
- Keep related pages internally linked so the page is not isolated.
- Avoid creating a new page when an existing page already owns the same intent.
- Prefer updating a page over fragmenting the site into multiple weak pages.

## Product or Feature Page

- Primary job: explain a capability and move the visitor toward trial, demo, signup, or deeper evaluation.
- Typical sections: hero, problem, capability breakdown, proof, how it works, objections or FAQ, CTA.
- Proof style: product evidence, screenshots, metrics, customer proof, implementation clarity.
- CTA style: direct and product-proximate.
- Internal links: pricing, demo, related features, use cases, docs.
- Risks: feature dumping, weak outcome framing, duplicate intent with solution pages.

## Landing or Campaign Page

- Primary job: convert traffic from one campaign, offer, or audience segment.
- Typical sections: tight hero, focused value proposition, proof, benefits, objections, CTA repeats.
- Proof style: concise and conversion-oriented.
- CTA style: one clear action, repeated strategically.
- Internal links: keep limited; avoid exit paths that dilute the campaign.
- Risks: trying to become a full site page, mixed CTAs, excess navigation noise.

## Solution or Use-Case Page

- Primary job: show how the offer solves a specific audience or scenario problem.
- Typical sections: audience context, pain, scenario-specific value, proof, workflow, CTA.
- Proof style: role- or industry-specific proof.
- CTA style: move from relevance to evaluation.
- Internal links: related features, customer stories, pricing, nearby solutions.
- Risks: sounding like a feature page with a different headline, weak audience specificity.

## Comparison or Alternative Page

- Primary job: help buyers evaluate choices and move them toward the preferred option.
- Typical sections: comparison framing, differences, strengths, limits, proof, FAQ, CTA.
- Proof style: concrete tradeoffs, credible comparisons, evidence-backed claims.
- CTA style: evaluation-oriented, often trial, demo, or migration step.
- Internal links: pricing, migration docs, feature detail, customer proof.
- Risks: dishonest framing, unsupported claims, legal or factual overreach.

## Help or FAQ Page

- Primary job: resolve questions quickly and reduce friction or support load.
- Typical sections: concise intro, grouped questions, direct answers, escalation path if needed.
- Proof style: clarity, accuracy, policy references, procedural confidence.
- CTA style: support-oriented first, conversion-oriented only when relevant.
- Internal links: docs, support, product pages, account or billing flows.
- Risks: invented questions, bloated answers, schema-first thinking without real utility.

### Dedicated FAQ vs In-Page FAQ

- Use an in-page FAQ when the questions directly support a product, pricing, landing, or comparison page.
- Use a dedicated FAQ or help page when the question set is broad enough to deserve its own destination.
- Keep in-page FAQ sections focused. Three to eight questions is the default range unless the site evidence justifies more.

## Integration or Plugin Page

- Primary job: show how the product connects to a platform or stack.
- Typical sections: hero, value of the connection, key workflows, proof or screenshots, setup clarity, CTA.
- Proof style: workflow outcomes, supported platforms, setup confidence.
- CTA style: connect, install, browse integrations, or request support.
- Internal links: docs, API, pricing, related integrations.
- Risks: vague ecosystem language, missing setup clarity, mixing catalog and single-integration intents.

## Evergreen Resource or Education Page

- Primary job: capture durable intent, educate, and route qualified visitors deeper into the site.
- Typical sections: answer-first intro, explanatory sections, examples, FAQ, contextual CTA.
- Proof style: useful detail, evidence, examples, expert framing.
- CTA style: lower pressure than a landing page; should fit the reader's stage.
- Internal links: cluster pages, product pages, related guides, help content.
- Risks: padding for SEO, no product connection, overlap with existing articles or guides.

## Structural Reminders

- Feature pages are capability-first.
- Solution pages are audience- or scenario-first.
- Landing pages are conversion-first.
- Help pages are resolution-first.
- Comparison pages are evaluation-first.
- Integration pages are connection-first.
- Evergreen resource pages are education-first.
